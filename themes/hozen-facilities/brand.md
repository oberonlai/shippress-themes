# Hozen Facilities - brand brief

Facilities management company for office campuses and civic buildings in the Rotterdam, Delft, The Hague and Dordrecht region, Netherlands. Sample content in English (stage 1: home page); Traditional Chinese, Japanese, French and German follow in stage 2. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Hozen Facilities B.V. The company looks after the plant rooms, roofs, risers and switchrooms of office campuses and civic buildings, so that the people inside never have to think about them.
- **Location:** Head office and 24/7 desk at Maashaven Oostzijde 21, 3072 HK Rotterdam, Netherlands (fictional). Hubs in Rotterdam, Delft, The Hague and Dordrecht. Euro prices in every language, excluding VAT; no converted amounts.
- **History:** Founded in 1998 in Rotterdam by mechanical engineer Ingrid Maas and electrical engineer Kwame Mensah, with one van and a plant room. The first campus contract (Maasoever Office Campus) followed in 2009, the Delft hub in 2014 and the Dordrecht hub in 2018.
- **Today:** 4 hubs, 23 contracted sites and 41 engineers. Every contracted site is within 25 minutes' drive of a hub; this is how the network is designed, not a promise of arrival. A 24/7 help desk answers every day of the year. Attendance times are contract targets and are always worded "target".
- **Audience:** facilities and estates managers, finance directors and building owners of office campuses, libraries, council buildings and sports centres who want the machinery behind a building looked after, logged and explained.
- **Voice:** Sound like a calm senior engineer on the phone: concrete, plain, willing to name the price and the limit. Short sentences, real numbers, no promises of results, no awards and no superlatives.

## Prices (EUR, excl. VAT; identical on every page and in every language)
1. Building survey & asset register: €1,850. One site up to 10,000 m2, every plant item tagged, written report within 10 working days.
2. Planned maintenance contract: from €2,400 / month, per site. Scheduled visits, filters, belts, readings, quarterly report.
3. 24/7 help desk: €310 / month, per site. Phone and portal logging, priority triage, closing notes.
4. Reactive call-out, weekdays 07:00-19:00: €95. First hour on site, travel included.
5. Out-of-hours call-out: €145. Evenings, nights, weekends, public holidays; first hour, travel included.
6. Additional engineer hour: €78, after the first hour, any time.
7. Statutory compliance programme: €3,200 / year, per site. Fire alarm and emergency lighting tests, water hygiene, lift and gas inspection coordination, logbook.

## Call-out priorities (contract targets)
- P1 Emergency (risk to people, flooding, total loss of power, heating or cooling): on site within 2 hours, 24/7.
- P2 Urgent (a system degraded, a floor uncomfortable): within 6 hours.
- P3 Routine (a fault with a workaround): within 3 working days.
- P4 Planned (improvements, small works): at the next scheduled visit.

## A normal day: how a call-out is taken
01 Call the desk or log it in the client portal. 02 The desk agrees the priority (P1 to P4) with you and texts a reference number within 10 minutes. 03 The nearest hub sends an engineer within the target time. 04 The engineer fixes or makes safe, and logs readings and photos to the site's asset record. 05 Your facilities contact gets a closing note the same day; if parts are needed, the return visit is booked before the engineer leaves.

## Signature techniques (carried to every page; reference: Nippon Kucho Service recruiting site, see brand/reference.md)
1. Staggered two-column photo pair (right column dropped 100 px) with caption rows, a 1 px rule, a dark arrow chip and 100 px ghost numerals #01 to #04.
2. Brand statement with a photograph that comes into focus (blur 10 px to sharp) and blurred ghost photographs at the margins.
3. Section lockup: Latin title, local label, a 1 px vertical rule and a short description on one row.
Supporting: a floating white header card with capsule pills, full-bleed photo bands with parallax, a contact cluster over a giant ghost word.
