# Kaoru Cocoa - brand brief

A chocolatier and cocoa cafe in the fictional Harrowfield that tempers small batches in the shop every morning and serves them the same afternoon. Sample content in English first; Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German follow, localised for each market. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Kaoru Cocoa. "Kaoru" is the Japanese word for the scent that rises from a pot: the shop is named for the smell of chocolate being tempered at ten in the morning. The shop itself is international and modern, with no Japanese setting.
- **Idea:** the marble slab is the stage. Everything on the menu is made in view of the seats, between ten and three, and served the same afternoon.
- **Location:** 14 Linden Arcade, Harrowfield (fictional). EUR prices including VAT in every language; no converted prices.
- **Founded:** 2017 by Noor Halvorsen. 24 seats; the marble slab stands four metres from the counter, behind a low glass screen.
- **Contact:** hello@kaoru-cocoa.example, +00 44 555 0173. Replies to orders and bookings within one working day.
- **Audience:** neighbours who drop in for a cocoa or a tasting flight, people booking a seated tasting for friends or a reading group, and anyone ordering a cake for a weekend celebration or small favours for an event.
- **Tone:** The tone is calm, unhurried, and grounded in the quiet precision of daily workshop routines. It speaks with specific, observational clarity about cocoa origins, tempering temperatures, and counter service without marketing embellishments or exaggerated claims. No guarantees, awards, press, health claims, real farms, real brands or customer counts.

## Story
Kaoru Cocoa was founded in 2017 by Noor Halvorsen at 14 Linden Arcade in Harrowfield. The workshop takes its name from the Japanese word for scent, chosen for the warm, deepening aroma of chocolate being tempered at ten o'clock each morning. From the first day, the premise was simple: to temper small batches of single-origin chocolate right in the shop every morning and serve them fresh across the counter the very same afternoon.

The room is arranged around a central white marble slab that stands four metres from the wooden counter, behind a low glass screen. Here, visitors in the twenty-four cafe seats can watch the steady progression of the making day as it unfolds. Tomás Rivera begins roasting and cracking beans at eight, Noor tables the tempered chocolate at ten, Lukas Brenner fills the moulds at noon, and Adaeze Okafor glazes the weekend cakes at three.

Working with beans from harvest growers at Alto Sereno, Río Claro Growers, Monte Ceiba, Bahía Verde, and Ridgeway Estate, the kitchen focuses entirely on snap, gloss, and immediate aroma. Chocolate is served within hours of tabling, whether poured as drinking cocoa, presented as tasting flights, or boxed by Sofia Marchetti for weekend collections. The shop remains quiet and deliberate, offering neighbourhood guests an unhurried window into daily artisan craft.

## How a cake order is placed
1. Choose a cake: Select Dark ganache 70%, Hazelnut praline, or Cocoa and pear in 18 cm or 22 cm, with an optional message for the plaque.
2. Pick a collection day: Choose Friday, Saturday, or Sunday between 11:00 and 17:00.
3. Order three days ahead: Submit via the order form, email, or at the counter; confirmed within one working day.
4. Collect: Boxed at 16:00 the day before and kept cool; paid at collection.

## A day at the shop (daily routine)
08:00 Tomas roasts and cracks the beans; 09:00 the doors open and the first pot of drinking cocoa is poured; 10:00 Noor tempers on the marble slab; 12:00 Lukas fills and taps the moulds; 13:00 tasting flights from today's batch begin at the counter; 15:00 Adaeze glazes the cakes for tomorrow's collections; 16:00 Sofia boxes the orders; 18:00 the shop closes. The routine is the same every working day (Tuesday to Sunday); only the daily batch changes.

## Making days of the week
Tuesday single-origin bars; Wednesday bonbons; Thursday bonbons and pralines; Friday cakes for the weekend; Saturday cake collection and seated tastings; Sunday tastings and cocoa only (no moulding); Monday closed.
