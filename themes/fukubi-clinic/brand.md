# Fukubi Clinic — brand

## Name
Fukubi Clinic (a digestive and liver outpatient clinic; "fukubi" echoes the soft belly of the body, said gently).

## Concept
Fukubi Clinic is a quiet outpatient gastroenterology practice that explains a digestive complaint before any test is booked. The home page is one calm page: a pale wash with five circular photographs and a week card showing which mornings are scope mornings, a symptom index, a walk through the first visit, the two-reader rule, fees and one booking call to action. Reference: Shinagawa Asada (a Japanese clinic: circle photo row, blurred halo, dot-matrix hours card, bowl-topped cards) and Taizankai (the thin sweeping curve under the hero); we borrow composition, technique, colour relations and motion, never photos, copy or logos.

## Place
Second floor, 48 Marlowe Street, Vancouver, BC V6B 2K9, Canada. A six-minute walk from Waterfront station. Fictional address.

## Founded
2014 by Dr Lena Moravec.

## History
Fukubi Clinic was founded in 2014 by Dr Lena Moravec on the second floor of 48 Marlowe Street in downtown Vancouver. Having practiced gastroenterology for years, Dr Moravec established the clinic around a simple clinical principle: a patient should understand their digestive complaint thoroughly before any invasive diagnostic procedure is scheduled. Situated just a six-minute walk from Waterfront station, the clinic was designed from the beginning as a quiet, dedicated outpatient facility for comprehensive digestive and liver care.

Over the past decade, the practice has grown into a focused team of three reading physicians, dedicated endoscopy nursing staff, a registered dietitian, and patient coordinators. The physical space reflects our methodical approach to outpatient medicine, housing three consultation rooms, a fully equipped endoscopy suite, and a daylight recovery lounge with four reclining chairs. Every consultation is structured to give patients unhurried time to review their medical history, discuss symptoms, and evaluate practical alternatives before considering procedural investigations.

Today, Fukubi Clinic remains committed to calm clarity and transparent clinical practice. We maintain a strict two-reader review policy for every endoscopy report, bill self-pay services in Canadian dollars with clear written estimates beforehand, and deliver all diagnostic findings in person rather than through impersonal letters. Whether managing chronic reflux, investigating persistent bowel changes, or assessing liver function, our team works together to ensure every patient leaves with an actionable understanding of their health.

## Audience
Adults with digestive or liver complaints (heartburn, stomach pain, bloating, changed bowel habits, blood in the stool, trouble swallowing, abnormal liver tests) and the people who come with them; referring family doctors.

## Voice
Our tone is calm, unhurried, and clinically precise, explaining digestive health in clear everyday English without medical jargon or dramatisation. We speak directly and transparently about procedures, fasting preparation, and fees, prioritising patient understanding and steady reassurance over promotional claims.

## A day at the clinic
A routine day starts at 7:30 on scope mornings (Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday), when patients who have fasted arrive to a calm preparation, and the endoscopy suite works until 12:30. Daily clinic hours are 8:00 to 12:30 and 14:00 to 18:30 on weekdays (Wednesday afternoon closed), with Saturday mornings only. Every report is read twice, and results are always explained in person at a follow-up visit.

## How the first visit is spent
- 0–10 min: Reception and registration — intake forms, medication list, and symptom diary review at the front desk.
- 10–35 min: Physician consultation — comprehensive medical history, physical examination, and initial diagnostic assessment.
- 35–45 min: Endoscopy nurse consultation — review of recommended tests, written cost estimate, scheduling, and fasting instructions.
- Follow-up: In-person results — diagnostic findings and biopsy reports are always explained in person at a scheduled follow-up consultation.
