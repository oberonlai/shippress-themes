# Teate Clinic: brand

## Name
Teate Clinic, a two-clinician osteopathy clinic (fictional) for desk workers and amateur athletes. Treatment is with the hands, not with machines. Industry: osteopathy clinic, business type; the home page tells a problem, method and proof story.

## Location and founding
14 Linden Row, Halbrook (fictional, international-neutral city), ground floor, step-free. Founded in 2014 by Maren Holt and Kofi Mensah. Email hello@teate-clinic.example, phone +00 44 555 0172.

## Story
Maren Holt and Kofi Mensah met while working in a sports-club clinic. The people who kept arriving were familiar: someone who sat all week and came in with a stiff neck, and someone who trained for fun and came in with a knee or an ankle that would not settle. In 2014 they opened Teate Clinic together at 14 Linden Row, Halbrook, a ground-floor room with a step-free entrance, a low couch and a window. They wanted a place that treated with the hands, not with machines.

The clinic has stayed small on purpose. There are two osteopaths, Inès Laurent at the front desk, and Ruth Adeyemi, who writes the clinic letter. The day starts at 07:45, when the rooms are aired and the couches are made with fresh linen. The first patient is at 08:00. At 12:30 the notes are written and Inès returns calls. Afternoon visits begin at 13:30. From 17:00 to 19:30 the evening slots are kept mainly for people coming from work. At 19:45 the laundry goes on and the rooms are reset.

What has not changed since 2014 is the pace of a visit. Ten minutes of talk, fifteen minutes of watching how you move, twenty-five minutes of hands-on treatment, and ten minutes for a plan and two or three exercises to take home. Many people need two to four follow-ups over a few weeks. Some need only one, and the clinic says so honestly. If something you tell them needs a doctor — a fever, numbness, night pain, or a fall — they stop and refer you. Teate Clinic is not a gym, and it does not diagnose disease.

## Audience
Desk workers with a stiff neck, shoulders or lower back, and weekend runners, cyclists and swimmers with a niggle in a knee, ankle or hip. People who want to be listened to first, treated with hands, and told honestly how many visits they are likely to need.

## Tone
Calm, concrete and unhurried, the way a small front desk speaks when it has time to listen. Name the hour, the price and the person, and never promise that a visit will make pain go away. No guarantees, no cure claims, no percentages, no awards, no real bodies or brands. The clinic does not diagnose disease and says when someone needs a doctor.

## A day at the clinic (daily routine)
Daily routine: 07:45 rooms aired and couches made with fresh linen; 08:00 first patient; 12:30 notes written and Inès returns calls; 13:30 afternoon visits; 17:00–19:30 evening slots for people coming from work; 19:45 laundry on, rooms reset.

## The first hour (60 minutes, same everywhere)
10 min talk (where it hurts, when, what makes it better or worse); 15 min watching how you move (standing, sitting, bending, walking); 25 min hands-on treatment; 10 min plan and two or three exercises to take home. Arrive five minutes early for a one-page form. Wear clothes you can move in; shorts for knees.

## The three complaints (same names on the home page and the Conditions page)
1. Neck and shoulders from desk work: stiffness, headaches at the end of the day, a shoulder that sits up.
2. Lower back: from long sitting, lifting, moving house, or a sudden twinge.
3. Sport niggles: knees, ankles, hips and shoulders for runners, cyclists and swimmers who train for fun.

## Visit flow
01 Book online, by phone or by email. 02 Fill in the one-page form on arrival (five minutes). 03 The first hour, as above. 04 A plan: many people need two to four follow-ups over a few weeks; some need only one, and we say so honestly.
