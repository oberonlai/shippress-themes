# Tochi Academy - brand brief

An evening college that prepares working adults for the real-estate licence exam, in the fictional Harbourside District of Westmere. Sample content in English first; Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German follow, localised for each market. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Tochi Academy. "Tochi" is the Japanese word for land; the college itself is international and modern, with no Japanese setting.
- **Location:** 14 Linden Quay, Harbourside District, Westmere (fictional). USD prices in every language; no converted prices.
- **History:** founded in 2009 by Ines Marlow, a practising conveyancer who saw that day jobs kept promising people from qualifying. She converted an unhurried waterfront building into a space for evening study only, with small cohorts and no shortcuts.
- **Today:** independent; seven people; cohorts capped at 18 seats; a 24-week term built on one rhythm of evenings, four timed mock papers, and one exam sitting at the end of each term.
- **Audience:** working adults who want the real-estate licence: career changers from other fields, juniors at letting and sales agencies, and returners after a career break or parental leave.
- **Tone:** calm, pragmatic and unhurried. Plain, specific sentences about statutes, timetables and desk work. No guaranteed results, pass rates, awards, press, ratings or student counts.

## A day at the college (daily routine)
12:00 the office opens; 18:30 doors open and the tea is on; 18:45 seminars begin; 20:00 the fifteen-minute tea break in the common room; 21:30 classes end; 21:45 the library closes. The daily routine is the same on every teaching evening: arrive, sit down, read the line closely.
