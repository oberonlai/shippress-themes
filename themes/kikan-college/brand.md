# Kikan College: brand

## Name
Kikan College, a plumbing and heating apprenticeship college for adults (fictional). One course, two years: one year at the bench in the college halls, one year placed with a working firm. Intake every September. Industry: apprenticeship college, business type.

## Location and founding
22 Foundry Lane, Wrenfield (fictional, international-neutral town). Founded in 2009 by Rosa Lindqvist and Emeka Obi in a rented foundry workshop with six benches. Email hello@kikan-college.example, phone +00 44 555 0193. Tagline: "Warm homes start at the bench."

## Story
Kikan College trains adults for site work in plumbing and heating. Rosa Lindqvist, a master plumber, and Emeka Obi, who spent two decades servicing boilers, founded it in 2009 and still teach. Year 1 is the bench year: 38 teaching weeks, September to July, four bench days (Monday to Thursday) in Bench Hall A (pipework, soldering, bending, drainage) and Bench Hall B (boiler rig, controls, heat pumps), and one theory day on Friday (water regulations, heat loss, reading drawings, safe working). Year 2 is the placement year: four days a week with a partner firm and one day at college on Friday, with one site visit per term from the placement coordinator, Grace Mwangi.

There are 24 places per intake (two groups of 12) and 18 placement partners within 40 km of Wrenfield. The minimum age is 18, there is no upper age, and no previous trade experience is needed. The college is not a quick certificate, not online, and Year 1 includes no gas work on live homes; Year 2 gas or electrical work happens only under the placement firm's qualified engineer. A placement is a training year, not a job offer. Many firms keep their apprentice on; the college never promises it.

## Audience
Adults who want a trade they can see working: career changers (a café manager, a warehouse worker), people returning to work after raising a family, and school leavers over 18 who want a bench rather than a lecture hall.

## Tone
Plain, specific and unhurried, like a good workshop instruction. Short sentences, concrete nouns (joint, bender, rig, gauge), numbers for times and prices. No guarantees, no invented statistics, no awards, no employment rates, no named qualifications or accreditation bodies.

## Hours
College office Mon–Fri 08:00–16:30. Bench halls Mon–Thu 07:45–15:30, Fri 08:30–15:30. Taster Saturday: first Saturday of every month 09:00–15:00 (not in August). Evening refresher: Tuesdays 18:00–21:00. Open bench visit: Tuesdays 16:00.

## A day at the college (daily routine)
Mon–Thu, the day strip (465 minutes): 07:45 tool check and toolbox talk (15); 08:00 bench block one, cut, bend, joint (135); 10:15 break (20); 10:35 bench block two, the week's piece (115); 12:30 lunch (45); 13:15 rig block, pressure test and fault-finding on the boiler rig (105); 15:00 logbook, sign-off, bay swept (30); finish 15:30. Friday theory day: 08:30–12:30 theory room (240); 12:30 lunch (45); 13:15–15:30 open bench and resits (135).

## Applying
01 Come to a taster Saturday or a Tuesday visit (16:00). 02 Apply online or by email; applications for September close 30 April. 03 A bench morning and a talk (90 minutes): you cut and join one piece of pipe; nobody expects it to be good. 04 An offer letter within 10 working days; a EUR 300 deposit holds the place and counts towards Year 1.
