# Michikusa Lodge: brand

## Name, place, founding
- **Name:** Michikusa Lodge. "Michikusa" is a Japanese word for stopping by the roadside on the way somewhere else; the lodge keeps that idea for a night. A single-storey motor lodge of 24 rooms.
- **Location:** Michikusa Lodge, Km 212, Old Trunk Road, Harlow Crossing (fictional, international-neutral).
- **Founded:** Built in 1987 by the Varga family as a roadside motel; rebuilt and reopened in 2016 by Ines Varga.
- **Line:** A quiet room off the trunk road.

## Story
Michikusa Lodge was built in 1987 by the Varga family as a simple roadside motel on the Old Trunk Road at Harlow Crossing, kilometre 212. Set along an international travel corridor, the single-storey motor lodge was designed with twenty-four rooms opening directly onto their own sheltered parking bays, giving long-distance drivers and late-night travellers a quiet place to pull off the road without delay.

In Japanese, michikusa means lingering or making an unhurried roadside detour on the way to somewhere else. In 2016, Ines Varga rebuilt and reopened the lodge around that quiet idea, preserving the single-storey layout while modernising every room, rebuilding the broad timber canopy, and installing six overnight vehicle charging bays. The purpose was to keep the roadside motor lodge practical, calm, and free from unnecessary ceremony.

Today, the canopy over the front entrance remains lit through every night of the year, welcoming travellers arriving long after dark. The front desk stays staffed until 02:00, automated night lockers hold keys for arrivals into the early morning, and fresh breakfast is served at the counter starting at 05:00. From late check-ins to dawn departures, the lodge provides quiet rooms, comfortable beds, and dependable rest beside the trunk road.
