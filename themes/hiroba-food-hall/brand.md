# Hiroba Food Hall - brand brief

A city food hall with eight independent kitchens under one roof, built around the weekday lunch. Sample content in English, Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German, localised for each market. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Hiroba Food Hall. "Hiroba" is the Japanese word for an open square; it is used as a name only, the hall is otherwise international and modern.
- **Location:** ground floor of a former harbour warehouse at Havenhof 12, 3011 AB Rotterdam, Netherlands (fictional address). EUR prices in every language; no converted prices.
- **History:** founded in 2019; the hall opened on 4 March 2019 with eight independent kitchens, 180 seats at long shared tables and a counter of 24 stools.
- **Audience:** office workers, colleagues and friends who want a calm, quick, shared weekday lunch; small groups of 6 to 12 who need one held table.
- **Tone:** The voice is calm, clear, and grounded in the practical rhythms of daily hall service, avoiding hospitality hype or decorative jargon. It addresses guests with direct, considerate precision, reflecting the practical hospitality of hall manager Ruth Adeyemi.
- **A day (the routine):** the hall opens at 11:00, the peak runs 12:10 to 13:00, the last lunch order is at 14:30, the hall resets at 15:00, the evening kitchens open at 17:00 and the last tables leave at 21:30. Saturday 12:00 to 21:30, Sunday closed.

## Story
Hiroba Food Hall opened on 4 March 2019 on the ground floor of a former harbour warehouse at Havenhof 12 in Rotterdam. The hall was established to bring eight independent specialist kitchens under one roof, organised entirely around the rhythm of the weekday lunch. Rather than dividing guests into separate dining rooms, the hall was arranged with one hundred and eighty seats across long shared tables, accompanied by a counter of twenty-four stools looking toward the open passes.

From the beginning, the hall was designed to solve the rush of midday dining in the city. Guests take trays along the central rail, choosing noodle broths, charcoal skewers, fresh pasta, or grain bowls from different counters, then sit together at communal timber tables. The concept allows colleagues and friends to combine plates from across the room on a single tray, turning lunch into a relaxed and shared routine rather than a hurried compromise.

Under the direction of hall manager Ruth Adeyemi, who has guided the floor since opening day, Hiroba maintains a calm and predictable schedule. The hall opens at 11:00, moves through its peak between 12:10 and 13:00, and quietens after the final lunch orders at 14:30 before resetting for the evening. Over the years in the harbour district, it remains an open square for uncomplicated food, generous space, and dependable weekday hospitality.
