## Story

Kamotsu Academy was founded in 2009 by Ruth Abernethy, a former fuel tanker driver with twenty-four years of commercial haulage experience. Operating initially from a single rented bay at Hollins Freight Park in Ashby Vale with one second-hand rigid lorry, Ruth established the school to offer structured, professional vocational instruction that reflected the demanding conditions of real commercial distribution.

Over the following years, the academy expanded steadily across Bay 4 to occupy a dedicated 1.4-hectare facility equipped with designated reversing bays, a full-size coupling area, and two classrooms for technical study. The fleet grew to five vehicles, comprising three 18-tonne rigid lorries and two tractor units paired with 13.6-metre curtain-side trailers, ensuring trainees work on modern, full-scale equipment.

Throughout this growth, the academy has maintained a strict training format of two trainees per lorry per day across six scheduled annual intakes. Instruction remains focused on deliberate routine and vehicle roadworthiness, insisting that every session begins with a methodical pre-drive walk-round check before any vehicle moves onto the road.

## Audience

Adults aged 18 and over who want a heavy-goods licence as a first or second career: warehouse staff, bus and van drivers, returners after years away from the cab, and the employers and families who ask how the academy teaches.

## Voice

The tone is calm, direct, and grounded in the daily routines of a working haulage yard. It communicates practical facts, vehicle safety, and thorough preparation without promotional hype or inflated promises. The words are plain and exact: "most trainees", "we aim to", never percentages or guarantees.

## A day (daily routine)
A typical yard day runs to the same daily routine: walk-round check at 07:30, manoeuvres at 08:00, road driving at 10:30, lunch at 12:30, the reversing exercise at 13:15, debrief at 16:00 and close at 17:00. Every lesson starts with the walk-round before the lorry moves.

## Course process (seven stops)
1. Week 0: Assessment drive (2 hours)
2. Weeks 1–2: Medical (D4 form) and provisional entitlement, plus two theory coaching evenings
3. Week 3: Theory and hazard perception tests passed, practical test date confirmed for week 7
4. Week 4: Yard week covering walk-round checks, coupling, and reversing exercise (2 days)
5. Week 5: Road days covering town, dual carriageway, and rural driving (2 days)
6. Week 6: Mock test conducted with a second instructor (1 day)
7. Week 7: Test day with warm-up drive followed by the practical test in our lorry
