A small mountain lodge on Harrow Ridge that keeps five rooms above the weather. Sample content in English (this release), later Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German, localised for each market. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Story
Kumoi Lodge stands at 1,640 metres along Harrow Ridge, high above the quiet expanse of the Vell valley. The original structure was built in 1931 as a rough timber herders' hut, offering essential shelter against harsh alpine storms for those moving livestock across the ridge. For decades, its stout log walls and stone chimney provided a safe haven when mountain weather turned without warning.

In 1964, the shelter opened its doors as a mountain lodge, welcoming walkers and travellers seeking refuge above the cloud line. Five simple timber rooms were established under the eaves, alongside a communal dining hall centered around a cast-iron wood stove. The lodge developed a tradition of shared evening suppers, hot tea for tired boots, and quiet rest before setting out across the ridge trails.

The present keepers took stewardship of Kumoi Lodge in 2009, maintaining its calm rhythms and historic character. Today, the lodge operates through two distinct seasons, keeping three rooms open in November before winter fully arrives. With warm beds, fresh sourdough baked each morning, and the outdoor tub steaming in the dusk air, the keepers ensure the fire is always lit for those coming in out of the weather.
