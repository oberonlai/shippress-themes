# Yubi College: brand

## Name, place, founding
- **Name:** Yubi College ("yubi" means finger). A small independent college that trains working adults to interpret between spoken language and sign language in hospitals, courts and schools. Deaf tutors teach every language class.
- **Location:** 3 Weaver's Yard, Calloway Street, Esterhaven. Esterhaven is a fictional harbour city.
- **Founded:** 1996 by Johanna Reiss, a Deaf teacher, and Tobias Mendel, a hospital interpreter, who began with a Saturday class of eleven adults in the meeting room of the Calloway Street public library.
- **Line:** The hands that carry the words.
- **Method:** you keep your job; you learn on two weekday evenings and Saturdays, language with a Deaf tutor in a group of nine, interpreting in pairs in the lab, and every Saturday a hospital, court or school scene with role-players, talked through at the table the same afternoon.

## Story
In 1996, Johanna Reiss, a Deaf teacher, and Tobias Mendel, a hospital interpreter, began teaching a Saturday class of eleven adults in the meeting room of the Calloway Street public library in Esterhaven. They saw that Deaf patients, defendants, and parents were regularly attending critical appointments without qualified interpreting, while working adults who wished to learn had no accessible training that fit around their jobs. Their goal was straightforward: to establish a rigorous, practice-led college where working adults could learn to carry words accurately between spoken language and sign language.

From that first Saturday class, Johanna and Tobias established the foundational principles that still guide Yubi College today. Every language class was placed under the guidance of a Deaf tutor, ensuring students learned language as it is lived and signed by the Deaf community. Practical training was rooted in realistic settings, moving students beyond vocabulary lists into simulated outpatient clinics, courtroom hearings, and classroom lessons. As enrolment grew, the college moved into 3 Weaver's Yard, converting a historic brick weaving shed into purpose-built facilities, including a booth room with six interpreting booths, two language rooms, and a long-table seminar room.

Today, Yubi College remains an independent college dedicated to training working adults without requiring them to leave their careers. Led by principal Helena Varga, who took leadership in 2015 after twenty-two years as a hospital and conference interpreter, the college partners with eleven hospitals, courts, and schools across Esterhaven. Decades after Johanna and Tobias gathered their first eleven students in the public library, the college continues to prepare interpreters who are trusted in the rooms where critical conversations take place.

## Audience
Nurses and care staff, school staff, people who grew up signing with Deaf family and people changing career: working adults with a job they keep, who want a disciplined qualification that fits around evenings and Saturdays. No signing is needed to start the Foundation Year.

## Tone
The voice is calm, clear, and grounded in practical reality, speaking with the measured confidence of experienced practitioners. It avoids advertising hype, empty promises, and emotional exaggeration, focusing instead on specific routines, partner settings, and the discipline of interpreting.

## A teaching week (the weekly routine, Interpreter Diploma, year two)
1. Tuesday 18:30–21:00, language with a Deaf tutor: nine people in a circle of chairs; no voices for the first hour.
2. Wednesday, two hours at home: three recorded dialogues to voice and sign back, logged in the practice book.
3. Thursday 18:30–21:00, the interpreting lab: in pairs in the booth room, one interprets, one watches and notes, then swap.
4. Saturday 09:30–12:30, a setting morning: a hospital, court or school scene played by role-players, a different setting each week.
5. Saturday 14:00–15:00, the table: the whole group talks the morning through with the tutors; notebooks closed.

Total: 10 hours of teaching a week, plus 2 at home. On a Saturday the daily routine runs from the setting morning to the table.
