# Mebuki College: brand

## Name, place, founding
- **Name:** Mebuki College ("mebuki" means the first buds of spring). A small independent college that trains early-childhood educators for nursery and reception classes.
- **Location:** 2 Orchard Row, Hollins, Calder Downs. Hollins is a fictional market town in the fictional Calder Downs.
- **Founded:** 1987 by Margit Sorensen, a nursery head who began with eight evening students in the back room of her nursery.
- **Line:** Learn the work beside the children.

## Story
Mebuki College was founded in 1987 by Margit Sorensen, an experienced nursery head who began teaching eight evening students in the back room of her nursery. From its beginning at 2 Orchard Row in the market town of Hollins, set within the Calder Downs, the college grew from the conviction that early-childhood educator training must take place alongside the living life of classrooms rather than in detached lecture halls. The name mebuki signifies the first buds of spring, reflecting the steady emergence of confidence in both young children and the adults who care for them.

Over four decades, the college developed an intensive apprenticeship model grounded in immediate practice. Every trainee spends two full days each week placed in a partner nursery or reception class starting from their third week of study. Rather than leaving students unassisted, a college practice tutor visits them on the floor once a week, observing interactions at children's eye level and sitting down that same afternoon to reflect upon what took place. This cadence connects child development theory with genuine nursery experience.

Today, Mebuki College remains deliberately small, welcoming cohorts of twelve to twenty-four students across three diploma and certificate routes. Fourteen partner nurseries and primary reception classes throughout Hollins collaborate closely with our faculty, ensuring trainees develop their craft in real rooms with diverse key groups. Tutors continue to work directly on the floor, preserving Margit Sorensen's founding principle: to learn the work of early-years education beside the children themselves.

## Audience
School leavers and career changers who want to work with children under five; graduates who want to teach nursery and reception classes; and classroom assistants who already work in early-years settings and want a qualification they can study for on Saturdays.

## Tone
The voice is calm, specific, and grounded in the daily reality of early-years rooms, avoiding promotional exaggeration or detached academic jargon. It speaks with quiet authority through unhurried sentences, concrete placement details, and direct respect for children and educators.

## Programmes and fees
- **Diploma in Early Years Practice**: 2 years full-time; seminars, studio, placements, weekly tutor observation. €6,400 per year.
- **Graduate Certificate in Early Years Teaching**: 1 year full-time for graduates; placements in nursery and reception. €7,200.
- **Weekend Route for Working Assistants**: 2½ years; Saturdays at college, practice in your own setting. €3,900 per year.
- **Application and interview**: Statement, reference check and the observed morning in a nursery. €40.
- **Placement kit (first year)**: Two aprons, observation notebooks, paediatric first-aid course. €180 once.
- **Short course: Sand, water and mud**: Four Saturdays with Owen Gallagher; open to anyone working with under-fives. €220.

A 300 euro deposit confirms a place and is counted toward the first year's fees. Fees can be paid in three instalments a year.

## A day on the course (the daily routine, Diploma, placement day)
08:30 Arrive at the partner nursery, greet the families at the door. 09:00 Floor practice with a key group of six children. 10:30 Your tutor watches from the doorway and takes notes. 12:00 Lunch at the low tables with the children. 13:30 Seminar at college: how children learn. 15:00 Studio: sand, water, music and movement. 16:15 Reflective journal, then close at 16:30.

Every trainee spends two days a week in a partner nursery or reception class from week 3, and a tutor watches them work once a week and talks it through the same afternoon.
