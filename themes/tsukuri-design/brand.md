## Story
Tsukuri Design Office was founded in 2011 by Ines Varga in Haverholt as an independent industrial and product design studio. Working from Studio 4 on Foundry Lane, the practice was established on a simple conviction: CAD renderings and paper specifications cannot reveal how an object feels when held in an exhausted hand or operated in the dark. From the beginning, the studio integrated a dedicated model shop directly alongside the design desks, shaping physical prototypes from high-density foam to test volume, balance, and tactile feedback before committing to tooling.

Over the years, the studio has maintained an intentional, disciplined scale of eight people, taking on at most nine projects a year and never running more than four in tooling simultaneously. This strict operational ceiling ensures that the same senior designers, researchers, and mechanical engineers stay engaged on every product from the first discovery workshop to the arrival of first-off production samples. Specialising strictly in medical devices and consumer products, the team balances rigorous human-factors documentation and regulatory usability files with lean, practical manufacturing constraints.

Today, our Haverholt workshop continues to bridge the critical gap between digital intent and factory execution. We spend weeks observing nurses in quiet hospital wards, patients managing chronic treatments at home, and assembly workers fitting components on high-volume factory lines. Whether testing dozens of hand-sanded handset prototypes or resolving draft angles and wall thicknesses for injection moulding, Tsukuri remains dedicated to producing durable, unpretentious physical goods that perform quietly and reliably under demanding everyday conditions.

## A day in the studio
The daily routine: 09:00 model review at the bench with Kofi, mornings of hand sanding and user sessions, afternoons in CAD with Mateo and Amara, Tuesday and Thursday model-shop visits from 10:00 to 16:00, and a short end-of-day shelf check where every foam model of the day is numbered and photographed.

## Method (four stages, research to tooling)
- 01 Research (weeks 1–4): We watch the product being used where it lives.
- 02 Foam (weeks 5–12): Dozens of hand-sanded foam models, held by real users.
- 03 Engineering (weeks 13–24): Wall thickness, draft angles, parts that click together.
- 04 Tooling (months 7–18): We stand next to the first-off samples at the factory.
