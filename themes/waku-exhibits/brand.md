# Waku Exhibits - brand brief

Exhibition stand builder and planner in the fictional port city of Lindhaven. A landing-page theme: one long home page with an anchor menu and a single repeated call to action, "Request a stand quote". Sample content in English, Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German, localised for each market. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Waku Exhibits. "Waku" is the Japanese word for frame; the stand is a frame for a conversation, not a spectacle. The company is otherwise international and modern.
- **Location:** workshop and office at Unit 4, Harbour Works, 12 Dock Road, Lindhaven, LH 2041 (fictional). Prices in EUR in every language, no conversions.
- **Founded:** 2014 by Ines Marlow.
- **Hours:** Mon–Fri 08:30–17:30, workshop build days Sat by appointment. Phone +00 20 4100 7720, email quotes@waku-exhibits.example.
- **Audience:** exhibitors and event teams who need a stand for a trade fair hall, a conference foyer, a retail atrium or a touring programme, and want to know the build before they sign.
- **Tone:** Our voice is calm, clear, and documentary, treating exhibition stands as functional frames for human conversation rather than loud spectacles. We speak in practical, workshop-grounded English focused on tape measurements, joinery, and hall rules without marketing hyperbole. Never "best", "guaranteed" or results claims.
- **Reply promise:** a written quote within 5 working days after the planning hour (not a guarantee of outcome).

## History
Waku Exhibits was founded in 2014 by Ines Marlow at Unit 4, Harbour Works, along Lindhaven’s working dockland. The name waku comes from the Japanese word for frame, expressing our belief that an exhibition stand should serve as an honest frame for conversation rather than an overbuilt spectacle. Over eleven years of working in exhibition halls, we have developed a calm, workshop-led practice dedicated to precise joinery, modular aluminium structures, and direct floor planning.

Our practice was established at 12 Dock Road to resolve the persistent gap between architectural drawings and hall-floor conditions. Rather than discovering layout conflicts on build day, we walk footprints with tape alongside our clients. Before any component travels to a venue, workshop foreman Samuel Okoye conducts a full workshop pre-build, standing up the complete structure in Lindhaven to test every structural connection, panel fit, and electrical route.

Today, our eight-person team plans and builds stands for trade fair halls, conference foyers, retail atriums, and multi-city tours across three to eight shows a year. Whether configuring an eighteen-square-metre row stand or engineering an island stand of seventy-two square metres, we maintain a transparent, fixed process. Every project begins with a sixty-minute planning hour and results in a clear written quote within five working days.

## A day at Harbour Works
A workshop day starts at 08:30 with Samuel Okoye's morning round: which panels are being cut, which crates are packed, which stand is standing in the pre-build bay. Ines Marlow or Lena Vasquez are usually out on a hall floor walking a footprint with orange tape; Mei Lin Hart at the client desk books the next planning hours; Priya Nair checks power and lighting against hall limits; Aiko Brandt proofs graphics. By 17:30 the tape plans are scanned, the day's quotes are in draft, and the crates for tomorrow's build are loaded. Saturdays are workshop build days by appointment.

## The planning hour (5 steps, shown inline)
1. Request: Submit your venue, dates, and stand dimensions via our two-minute inquiry form.
2. Planning hour: Spend sixty minutes walking your stand footprint on the hall floor or over video with the hall plan.
3. Written quote: Receive a fixed, line-item written quote within five working days after the planning session.
4. Workshop pre-build: We fully erect and inspect your stand in our Lindhaven workshop before transportation.
5. Hall build & dismantle: Our crew handles complete on-site construction, technical handover, and post-show dismantling.

Time split of the 60 minutes: 15 min hall plan and rules, 20 min walking the footprint with tape, 15 min materials and reuse, 10 min budget and dates.

## Signature techniques (carried to every page)
1. Photo-filled statement: a two-line 900-weight Onest statement whose letters are filled with a hall-floor photograph (source: Hakuten vision statement).
2. Edge-flush alternating rows: photos that touch the viewport edge, alternating right and left, with a 404 px text column, hairline and #tags (source: Hakuten works rows), under a giant section word cut by the section top.
3. Two flat white cards on a full-bleed photograph: the quote request and the early-access list (source: Hakuten contact band).
4. Supporting: a fixed 1 px right rail with a travelling orange dot, a 64 px outline circle menu, halftone lilac dots drifting 15 px.
Key visual: a documentary photograph of a planner pointing at orange tape on the hall floor of a half-built stand while the client holds the drawing; an orange tape line draws across the lower edge once on load.
