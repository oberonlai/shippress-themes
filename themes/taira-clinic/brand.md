# Taira Clinic — brand brief

Design plan row: b42-1 (vestibular and balance clinic, type business). Stage one: the home page in English. Inner pages and the other languages follow. All names, places, people and figures are fictional. Clinical copy never promises a cure or an outcome.

## Concept
- **Name:** Taira Clinic, a small vestibular and balance clinic.
- **Location:** 14 Linden Row, Harbourside, Northgate (fictional). Phone +1 (555) 014-2200. Email desk@taira-clinic.example.
- **Founded:** 2011 by Dr Lena Ortiz.
- **Audience:** adults who are spinning, unsteady or dizzy and cannot name a department that treats it; people after a head knock, older people who have fallen or nearly fallen, and the relatives who bring them.
- **Tone / voice:** Calm, specific and clinical, the way a small clinic talks to a person who is already unsteady. Short sentences, real rooms and named steps, with no promises, awards or borrowed authority.

## Story
Taira Clinic opened in 2011 as a vestibular and balance clinic at 14 Linden Row, Harbourside, Northgate. Dr Lena Ortiz founded it so people with spinning, unsteadiness and dizziness could be measured and retrained in one room, rather than passed between departments that treat balance as a side issue. The clinic stays small on purpose: seven clinicians, a foam pad, a gaze chart of plain dots and arrows, video goggles and a balance platform, and time enough for a sixty-minute first assessment.

A typical day starts at 08:00. Priya Nair runs gaze and positional tests while Tomas Weller, Amara Osei and Jonah Lindqvist work through retraining on the pad and in the corridor. Sofia Marchetti keeps the book and the first-visit path clear. Daniel Achebe runs the desk, the ramp and the Saturday morning list. Patients arrive with a symptom they can name — rolling over in bed, a crowded station, a head knock, a near-fall, a supermarket aisle — and leave with a plan they agreed in the room.

The method is the same three moves for everyone who is safe to treat here: measure, retrain, return. Sessions are usually six, spread over eight to twelve weeks, with two short exercises at home on the days between. The clinic does not promise a result. Outcomes differ from person to person. What it does offer is a calm path: find the symptom or the test, book the first assessment online or by phone, and walk the plan back toward the street, the market, the quiet road or the shop.

## People
- **Dr Lena Ortiz** — Founder, vestibular physician. Dr Lena Ortiz opened Taira Clinic in 2011 so dizziness and unsteadiness could be assessed in one place. She leads the medical side of the first assessment and the letter back to a patient’s own doctor.
- **Tomas Weller** — Lead vestibular physiotherapist. Tomas Weller leads the retraining work on the foam pad and in the corridor. He sets the session plan after the tests and checks how the home exercises are landing.
- **Amara Osei** — Physiotherapist, falls prevention. Amara Osei works with people whose balance problem shows up as falls or near-falls. She runs the falls-risk screen for people over 65 and the practice that follows it.
- **Priya Nair** — Clinical audiologist, gaze and positional testing. Priya Nair runs the video gaze test and the positional test. She is the person who sits with the goggles and the laptop while the eyes are recorded.
- **Jonah Lindqvist** — Physiotherapist, post-concussion care. Jonah Lindqvist sees people whose dizziness followed a head knock, including cyclists and others back from an injury. He builds the slower return that comes after a concussion.
- **Sofia Marchetti** — Patient coordinator. Sofia Marchetti is the first voice on the booking line and the person who walks a new patient through what to bring. She keeps first assessments inside the usual five working days when the book allows.
- **Daniel Achebe** — Clinic manager. Daniel Achebe runs the clinic day: hours, the ramp entrance, Saturday mornings and the desk. He is the manager patients meet if a plan, a receipt or a cancellation needs sorting.

## Patient path
1. Book online or call the desk.
2. First assessment, 60 minutes.
3. Plan agreed together.
4. Sessions, typically six over 8–12 weeks, plus daily home exercises.
5. Review and a letter to your own doctor.

Method: **Measure · Retrain · Return.**
