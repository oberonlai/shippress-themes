# Hisagi Neurology - brand brief

An outpatient neurology clinic that explains a symptom before it orders a scan, in Westmere, a fictional city. Sample content in English, Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German, localised for each market. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Hisagi Neurology. A clinic where the history comes first, the examination is done by hand, and a scan is ordered only when its result would change the plan.
- **Location:** 2nd floor, 14 Linden Row, Westmere (fictional). Tram line 4, stop Linden Row, 2 minutes' walk. Telephone 010 482 0116, email hello@hisagi-neurology.example. Prices in euro.
- **Founded:** 2012 by Dr Ines Halloran, with two consulting rooms.
- **Hours:** Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri 08:30-17:30; Wed 11:00-19:30 (evening clinic); Sat 09:00-13:00 (follow-ups and tests only); Sun and public holidays closed. Phone line Mon-Fri 08:30-12:30. Case meeting daily at 08:00.
- **Audience:** adults referred by a GP or booking directly with a new or unexplained neurological symptom, and families of older patients.
- **Tone:** calm, plain and explanatory, the way a clinician speaks once the door is shut. Never alarming, never promising results, no invented statistics.
- **One call to action (everywhere):** "Book a consultation" (first visit, 60 min, EUR 180).

## History
Hisagi Neurology was founded in 2012 by Dr Ines Halloran at 14 Linden Row, Westmere, on the second floor, with two consulting rooms. It is an outpatient clinic for adults who have a new or unexplained neurological symptom, whether a GP has referred them or they book directly, and for families of older patients. A first visit lasts 60 minutes: 40 minutes for the history and 20 minutes for an examination by hand. The written plan follows within 5 working days, and a copy goes to the GP.

In 2016 a neurophysiology room was added, so a nerve conduction study and a routine EEG can be recorded in the same building, each with a report. In 2021 the headache programme began, a first visit with two follow-ups and a diary review over 12 weeks, and The Case Letter started with it. The letter goes out on the first Tuesday of each month. It explains one symptom, includes one question from a patient, and carries clinic notes such as holiday hours. It takes about 4 minutes to read and has no advertising.

The day still opens with a case meeting at 08:00, and the first patients are seen at 08:30. Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday run from 08:30 to 17:30. Wednesday is the evening clinic, from 11:00 to 19:30. Saturday, 09:00 to 13:00, is for follow-ups and tests only, and Sunday and public holidays are closed. The phone line is open Monday to Friday, 08:30 to 12:30, and letters are written the same afternoon. The working order has stayed the same since the rooms opened: listen before looking, examine by hand, and scan only when the answer would change the plan.

## The first visit (four steps)
1. Book by phone or the form; a short questionnaire arrives 3 working days before. First appointments are usually offered within 3 weeks.
2. History, 40 minutes, in your own words.
3. Examination, 20 minutes, by hand: reflexes, strength, sensation, coordination and gait.
4. Written plan within 5 working days, with a copy to your GP.

## The three principles
01 Listen before we look. 02 Examine by hand (a reflex hammer, a tuning fork, a pen and paper). 03 Scan only when the answer would change the plan, and say why in writing.

## Cases (names changed)
1. **A cup that shook** - Ruth, 64, retired teacher, afraid of Parkinson's; the tremor appeared on action, not at rest; the spiral drawing and family history pointed to essential tremor; no scan; a plan with her GP; after 10 weeks she writes letters again (home page case).
2. **Headaches every morning** - Daniel, 38; a 6-week diary showed painkiller overuse; the headache programme with a gradual reduction.
3. **Numb fingers at night** - Amara, 51; the nerve conduction study confirmed carpal tunnel syndrome; referred to a hand surgeon, no brain scan needed.
