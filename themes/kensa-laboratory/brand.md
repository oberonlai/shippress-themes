# Kensa Laboratory — brand

## History
Kensa Laboratory opened in 2009 as a materials testing bench for concrete, rebar, cores and chemistry. The work sits between the pour and the decision: a builder sends a specimen, the lab records what it bears, and a signed PDF report goes back two working days after the test. The bench was set up so that the person who mixed the concrete is not the person who writes the number. From the first year the open list was the same shape it is now — compression, flexure, slump and air, rebar tensile and bend, cores, density and absorption, chloride content, and a custom test plan when the eight named tests do not cover the question.

In 2017 the laboratory moved to Unit 4, 18 Harbour Works Road, Northgate. The Harbour Works rooms hold the compression machine, the flexural frame, the curing tank and the chemistry bench in one receiving sequence. Samples come in through the bay between 08:30 and 16:00 on weekdays. Each one is logged the hour it arrives, marked with its submission number and cast date, and held for the due test date. Saturday receiving, 09:00–12:00, is by arrangement. The laboratory is closed on Sunday and on public holidays. Lab hours on weekdays run 08:00–17:30.

Six people work the bench. Ingrid Valen directs the laboratory. Tomás Ferreira leads concrete. Amara Okafor runs metals and rebar. Priya Nair runs chemistry. Marcus Lindqvist takes site coring. Lena Hoffmann runs sample reception. Reports are written to recognised standard methods, with EN or ASTM equivalents named on the report. Express reporting is available in 24 hours at 40 percent above the listed fee. Specimens are kept for 30 days after the report, then released. Kensa Laboratory does not make accreditation claims, award citations or promises about site performance. It reports what the sample did under the test that was booked.

## Audience
Site engineers, contractors, structural consultants and building owners who need a written test result on concrete, rebar, cores or chemistry before they decide, and the project managers who book and send the samples.

## Voice
Calm, specific and documentary: short sentences, named tests, named people and the numbers on the fee card, with no flourish and no promise the bench cannot keep. Write as a working laboratory speaking to a site engineer who already knows what a cylinder is.

## People
- **Ingrid Valen** — Lab director. Ingrid Valen directs Kensa Laboratory and signs the written reports that leave the bench. She sets the due date for each booked submission and keeps the open test list to the eight tests on the card.
- **Tomás Ferreira** — Concrete lead. Tomás Ferreira leads concrete testing, from compressive strength on cylinders to flexural strength on beams. He checks specimen size and cast date before a set goes into the machine.
- **Amara Okafor** — Metals & rebar. Amara Okafor runs rebar tensile and bend tests, one bar at a time in the grips. She records the bar as it was received and writes the result against that mark.
- **Priya Nair** — Chemistry. Priya Nair runs chloride content on powdered concrete and the chemistry side of a custom test plan. She prepares each sample in glass and ties the result to the submission number.
- **Marcus Lindqvist** — Site coring. Marcus Lindqvist extracts cores on site and brings them back for test. He drills where the booking names the location and labels each core before it leaves the slab.
- **Lena Hoffmann** — Sample reception. Lena Hoffmann receives samples at the bay and logs each one the hour it arrives. She checks the submission number and the cast date on the label before the specimen goes to the bench.

## Submission process
1. Book a submission online and receive a submission number by email.
2. Label and pack each specimen with the submission number and the cast date.
3. Drop off at receiving between 08:30 and 16:00, or book a courier pickup.
4. Test on the due date. The written report follows 2 working days later.
