# Tomari Marina — brand brief

Design plan row: b22-4 (marina and berth operator, hospitality). English home and inner pages are built (T1 page heads, T2 boards, T3 bottom line on every inner page); zh-TW, ja, fr, de follow. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Tomari Marina.
- **Location:** Harbour Office, 2 North Quay, Wrenhaven. Wrenhaven is a fictional, internationally neutral coastal town with one working quay.
- **Founded:** 1987, by Rolf Brandt. The pontoons were rebuilt in 2004; Lucie Brandt has run the marina since 2019.
- **Size:** 100 berths: Pontoon A 36 finger berths up to 7.5 m, Pontoon B 28 berths 7.5–10 m, Pontoon C 14 berths 10–12 m, and 22 swing moorings up to 9 m in the outer basin.
- **Audience:** owners of small sailing and motor boats who want a named berth for the season, visiting boats, and owners who winter ashore.
- **Tone:** The voice is calm, concrete and slightly dry, the way a harbour office speaks to owners who already know their own boats. It names the pontoon, the hour and the fee in EUR, and it keeps every sentence inside the working day.
- **Hours:** April–October, daily 07:00–19:00; November–March, Monday–Saturday 08:00–16:00. VHF channel 12.

## Story
Tomari Marina opened in 1987 on the North Quay of Wrenhaven, a small coastal town with one working quay. Rolf Brandt laid out a small-boat harbour for owners who wanted a finger they could walk to and a person on the pontoon in the morning. The harbour office has stood at 2 North Quay from the start. He kept the place to a single basin: finger pontoons for the boats already using this stretch of quay, and swing moorings in the outer water. The morning round became the ordinary working day, and the marina has stayed that size of harbour ever since.

The pontoons were rebuilt in 2004. Pontoon A was set out with 36 finger berths for boats up to 7.5 m, Pontoon B with 28 berths for 7.5–10 m, and Pontoon C with 14 berths for 10–12 m. A further 22 swing moorings in the outer basin take boats up to 9 m. Together they make 100 berths. Water and 16A power run along A, B and C. Depth at low water is 2.0 m on A, 2.4 m on B, 3.0 m on C and 3.5 m on the moorings. The rebuild put new pontoons in the same harbour. The count remained 100 berths.

Lucie Brandt has run the marina since 2019. Rolf Brandt stays in the harbour story as its founder. The season is still 1 April to 31 October. Requests open on 10 January. An owner walks the berth with dockmaster Tomas Reyes, signs at the office, and pays a 25% deposit, with the balance due by 1 March. Harbour master Ines Marlow walks the round at 06:30 every day of the season, about 50 minutes from the fuel berth to the outer moorings. The office answers on VHF 12, and the pontoon key is handed over on the first round. The crew can still name the boats they pass.

## People
- **Lucie Brandt** — owner. Lucie Brandt has run Tomari Marina since 2019. She keeps the season to 1 April–31 October and the harbour to 100 berths on the North Quay.
- **Ines Marlow** — harbour master. Ines Marlow walks the morning round at 06:30 every day of the season, about 50 minutes from the fuel berth to the outer moorings. She is the person on the pontoon with the clipboard at first light.
- **Tomas Reyes** — dockmaster, berths and allocations. Tomas Reyes allocates fingers on Pontoons A, B and C and the swing moorings in the outer basin. He walks each requested berth with the owner before the season agreement is signed.
- **Ada Okonkwo** — office and bookings. Ada Okonkwo takes season requests from 10 January at the harbour office, 2 North Quay. She keeps the bookings list through to the 25% deposit and the balance due by 1 March.
- **Henrik Vale** — yard and travel lift. Henrik Vale runs the yard and the 25 t travel lift. Lift-out, wash and block-off, and winter hard standing from November to March, sit with him.
- **Samir Haddad** — fuel berth and pump-out. Samir Haddad staffs the fuel berth and pump-out, where the 06:30 round begins. In the season he is there through the office day, 07:00–19:00.
- **Mei Lin Choi** — evening watch. Mei Lin Choi keeps the evening watch through the season. She makes the 21:00 check once the morning round is long finished.

## A day at the marina (the daily routine)
- 06:30 Ines Marlow starts the morning round at the fuel berth and walks Pontoon A, B and C to the outer moorings, about 50 minutes.
- 07:00 The office opens (April–October, daily); Samir Haddad opens the fuel berth and pump-out.
- Daytime: Tomas Reyes walks berths with owners, Ada Okonkwo keeps the bookings, Henrik Vale works the yard and the 25 t travel lift.
- 21:00 Mei Lin Choi makes the evening check.

## Signature
A berth is a relationship, walked each morning. One full-bleed low-key photograph of the harbour master and an owner on a pontoon opens the page; a pinned three-step frame (pontoon, round, season) tells which berths, how the morning round is walked and how a season is booked; a booking-led close ends it.
