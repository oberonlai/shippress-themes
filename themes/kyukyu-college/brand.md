## Story

Kyukyu College was founded in 2011 at 41 Wexford Lane in Northmere by Dr. Ruth Adeyemi-Holm alongside two veteran station paramedics. Observing that pre-hospital education often separated textbook theory from the chaotic physical reality of roadside emergency care, they established an independent institution dedicated entirely to the critical first eight minutes of emergency response. The college began with a singular focus on practical, immersive scenario work supported directly by foundational clinical science.

From its inception, the campus was designed around dedicated practical environments rather than traditional lecture halls. The Northmere facility brought together two focused classrooms, a fully equipped drill hall for vehicle extrication exercises, and a specialized skills lab housing two realistic ambulance-body simulators. This infrastructure ensured that cohorts could practice high-stress clinical procedures, stretcher handling, and monitored cardiac compressions under authentic spatial constraints from their earliest weeks of study.

Over the past decade, the college has maintained deliberate restraint in its scale, limiting each intake to cohorts of twenty-four students. This structure preserves an eight-to-one student-to-instructor ratio in the skills lab and ensures that every learner receives rigorous direct supervision from clinical instructors who still work active ambulance shifts. Today, the college continues to prepare disciplined emergency medical technicians and paramedics through thirty-six-week academic cycles paired with four hundred and eighty hours of operational frontline ride-along placements.

## Audience

Adults aged 18 and over choosing paramedicine as a first or second career, career changers in their thirties and forties, and the families and employers who ask how the college teaches.

## Voice

The voice is calm, clear, and grounded in the quiet discipline of emergency clinical education. It speaks directly without dramatic embellishment, commercial hype, or unearned promises, focusing entirely on practical skill, rigorous hours, and patient care.

## A day (daily routine)

A routine day at the college starts in the skills lab at 07:30. Classroom terms run seminars in the morning and scenario drills in the afternoon; each drill ends with a timed debrief. In the ride-along term the day is a twelve-hour shift (07:00–19:00 or 19:00–07:00) with a paramedic mentor, followed by a reflective log; Friday mornings return to the college for a debrief with a tutor.

## Admission process

1. Apply by 31 March for the September intake or 30 September for the February intake with the $60 application fee
2. Written test administered in April (September intake) or October (February intake)
3. Physical fitness and manual handling assessment including a weighted carry-chair lift and 3-minute step test
4. Formal interview with clinical faculty in May
5. Health clearance, mandatory occupational vaccinations, and criminal background check completed in June
