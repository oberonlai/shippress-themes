# Hibari Flight - brand brief

Private pilot flight school on a regional airfield. Sample content in English, Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German, localised for each market. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Hibari Flight School. "Hibari" is the Japanese word for the lark, the bird that climbs straight up from open grass; the school is otherwise international and plain-spoken.
- **Location:** Hangar 2, Brackmoor Airfield, Field Road, Alder Vale (fictional). USD prices in every language; no converted prices.
- **Founded:** 2011, by Margit Halvorsen (head of training) and Ruth Abernathy (chief engineer), with two light training aircraft in Hangar 2.
- **Audience:** adults who want to learn to fly for pleasure or as a first step towards a licence: first-time visitors booking a trial flight, students working through the PPL or LAPL, and pilots adding a night rating.
- **Tone:** The voice is calm, clear, and unhurried, reflecting the steady professionalism of practical aviation instruction without technical jargon or marketing hype. It emphasizes hands-on experience, atmospheric awareness, and transparent pricing in natural, grounded English.
- **No claims of** pass rates, awards, guaranteed timelines, real regulators' endorsement, real airfields or real people.

## History
Hibari Flight School was founded in 2011 by Margit Halvorsen and Ruth Abernathy at Brackmoor Airfield in Alder Vale. Setting up operations in Hangar 2 with two light training aircraft, Margit served as head of training while Ruth oversaw maintenance as chief engineer. Their goal from the outset was to create a calm, disciplined flight school rooted in genuine airmanship and regional grass-roots flying, away from the rushed environment of commercial flight academies.

Over the following years, the school steadily grew while maintaining its focused, personal atmosphere on the airfield. Hangar 2 expanded its fleet to include three two-seat low-wing trainers dedicated to ab-initio instruction and one four-seat touring aircraft for cross-country navigation exercises. Additional instructors joined the roster to lead ground-school courses, night ratings, and advanced navigation, all sharing the core conviction that every safe pilot first learns to understand the local weather and grass runway conditions.

Today, Hibari Flight School continues to operate from Brackmoor Airfield along runway 09/27, using both the 780-metre grass strip and the 640-metre asphalt surface. Students train six days a week from early morning until sunset, flying dual lessons, conducting thorough pre-flight walk-rounds, and mastering cross-country journeys across the Vale. More than a decade after its first flight, the school remains committed to patient, one-to-one flight instruction where reading the wind comes first.

## A day at the field (the daily routine)
Flying runs Tuesday to Sunday from 08:00 to sunset; the office is open Tuesday to Saturday, 09:00-17:00; Mondays are closed. A typical day starts with the instructors reading the windsock and the forecast, then lesson blocks follow one another on runway 09/27: brief, walk-round, fly, debrief. In the evenings of the ground-school term (Tuesday and Thursday 19:00-21:00) the clubhouse is a classroom. The routine of every lesson is the same shape, so a new student always knows what comes next.

## Lesson block (2 hours on the field)
1. Brief (25 min): Review the weather forecast, discuss circuit traffic, and set specific learning objectives for the sortie.
2. Walk-round (20 min): Conduct a thorough external inspection of the airframe, control surfaces, fuel samples, and propeller.
3. Fly (60 min): Execute the flight lesson from runway 09/27, focusing on aircraft handling, navigation, or circuit landings.
4. Debrief (15 min): Return to the apron, record flight times in the logbook, and review performance notes with your instructor.
