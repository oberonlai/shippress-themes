# Nohara Holiday Park - brand brief

A holiday park and touring caravan site for families: 52 pitches and 14 timber lodges on eleven hectares of oak and ash, kept between the trees, with a warden who walks every guest to their pitch.

## Concept
- **Name:** Nohara Holiday Park.
- **Location:** Ash Hollow Lane 1, Wenlow Vale (a fictional, international-neutral address). Telephone +00 31 6120 0841. Email hello@nohara-park.example. Prices in EUR (€) in every language.
- **Founded:** 2004, by Ingrid Halloran, on a former tree nursery.
- **Audience:** families, couples and cyclists who tow a caravan, bring a tent or want a timber lodge with the beds made; and people who store a caravan over winter.
- **Tone:** Calm, grounded, and practical, speaking with the quiet confidence of an established woodland holiday park. Factual and hospitable without promotional hype, superlatives, or invented accolades.
- **Hours:** In season daily 08:30–18:00; out of season daily 09:00–16:00; closed 25–26 December. Phone answered in reception hours; email answered within 1 working day. Pitches open 15 March to 2 November; lodges open all year.

## History
Ingrid Halloran founded Nohara Holiday Park in 2004 on the grounds of a former tree nursery along Ash Hollow Lane in Wenlow Vale. Rather than clearing the land for uniform rows of vehicles, she preserved the mature oak and ash trees, setting the first twelve gravel touring pitches into natural clearings where vehicles and awnings could rest sheltered between the trunks.

Over subsequent seasons, the park developed gently across its eleven hectares without disturbing its woodland character. The grounds grew to include thirty-six gravel touring pitches with 10A electric hook-ups, sixteen grass tent pitches along the meadow edge, and fourteen timber lodges built quietly under the canopy from 2012. A heated wash house, reception shop, and the 2.4-kilometre oak wood loop walk were added to support calm, self-contained stays.

Today, Nohara remains an independent holiday park cared for by a seven-person team who walk the grounds every morning. Ingrid still walks the oak wood trail at dawn, while wardens look after arrivals, maintenance, and seasonal conservation. The park continues to offer families and travelers quiet, uncrowded space under the trees from spring through winter.

## How a week is reserved (four ruled rows)
1. Ask: Choose dates by phone, email, or through the booking page, with Saturday changeover for pitch and lodge weeks.
2. Hold: Reception holds your chosen pitch or lodge for 48 hours free of charge while you confirm travel arrangements.
3. Confirm: Pay the 25% deposit to receive confirmation with your pitch or lodge number and park map within 1 working day.
4. Arrive: A warden meets you at the gate and walks you to your pitch; pitches arrive from 13:00, lodges from 15:00.

Fixed figures (identical everywhere): 48 hours hold, 25% deposit, Saturday to Saturday weeks, arrival from 13:00 (pitches) and 15:00 (lodges), departure by 11:00 and 10:00, quiet hours 22:00–07:00, 8 km/h on the park roads.

## A day at the park (daily routine)
The daily routine starts well before the gate opens at 08:30: Tobias Weller walks the gravel circuit at first light, checking water points and the 10A hook-ups, and visits the pond; Ingrid Halloran walks the oak wood loop. Reception opens, a warden meets each arrival at the gate and walks them to their pitch, and in the afternoon the wardens look after the lodges and the wash house. Saturday is changeover day, with pond mornings for children from April.

## Out of season (November to mid-March)
Hedge laying along the lane (November–December), drain and wash-house maintenance (January), tree survey and coppicing in the oak wood (February), re-gravelling the touring pitches (early March) and winter caravan storage for up to 30 caravans, checked every week and aired monthly.
