# Komugi School - brand brief

Professional bakery school for people moving into commercial kitchens. Sample content in English, Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German, localised for each market. All names, places, people, prices and contact details are fictional. Home page (stage 1) is English only.

## Concept
- **Name:** Komugi School. "Komugi" is the Japanese word for wheat; the school is otherwise international and modern (no Japanese elements in the imagery).
- **Location:** Hallstraat 14, 3011 KD Rotterdam, Netherlands (fictional address). EUR prices in every language, VAT included. Phone +31 10 555 0140, email hello@komugischool.example.
- **History:** In 2014 Elise Vandamme established Komugi School at Hallstraat 14 in Rotterdam to provide focused, practical vocational training for bakers on their way into commercial kitchens. The school began with a direct commitment: stripping away domestic baking shortcuts to instill manual dexterity, production rhythm and disciplined recipe formulation on commercial equipment.
- **Hours:** Teaching Mon-Fri 06:00-14:00; office Mon-Fri 09:00-17:00; open bench mornings Sat 09:00-12:00 (EUR 45, 8 places).
- **Audience:** career changers, home bakers and junior bakers who want a place in a professional bakery or to open their own.
- **Tone:** Calm, precise and understated, like the quiet focus of an early-morning production bakery. It speaks plainly about daily bench repetition, technical standards and real kitchen workflow. No guarantees of jobs, no accreditation claims, no awards.

## Story
Over the following decade the school became a dedicated teaching bakery with twelve individual student workbenches, four spiral mixers, three four-deck ovens and a retarder-prover room. Training is built around daily kitchen reality: students at the bench five mornings a week from six o'clock, working through foundational bread, viennoiserie and sourdough routines.

Today Komugi School stays deliberately small: five twelve-student cohorts a year, short courses and open bench mornings. Cohorts begin on 12 January, 9 March, 4 May, 7 September and 2 November. Each Professional Bread & Pastry course pairs six weeks of bench instruction with two further weeks in one of eight partner kitchens. The placement comes after the course and is not paid.

## Visual identity
- Reference: Japan Typography Association (typography.or.jp). Chip bar above one huge top-down photograph that bleeds off the right edge, an 82px left rail with a rotated credit, hairline list rows with 4:3 thumbnails, two opposite-direction name tickers, a solid 54px mark top-right.
- Colour: ground #FAFAFA, ink #14110E, chip / main 媚茶 #716454, hairline #DADADA, muted text #5C554D, wheat accent #A8845A (small marks only), footer #F1EFEC. No cream ground, no section filled in brown.
- Type: Fraunces (headings and body), Outfit (chips, nav, labels). Traditional Chinese and Japanese in Noto Serif TC / JP. Weight 400 throughout; 500 only for chips and buttons.
- Radius 0 on images, chips, cards; pill buttons 9999px. No shadows.
- Photography: documentary 35mm film, visible grain, warm near-monochrome, high-key window daylight, top-down on the bench. No text, no logos.
- Motion: slow parallax on the bench photograph and the production floor, 24px rise with stagger on the day rows, left-to-right clip wipe on the info bar and the statement, two tickers moving in opposite directions.
