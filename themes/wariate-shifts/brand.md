# Wariate Shifts - brand brief

Rota (shift-scheduling) software for independent shops, shown as a one-page demo-booking site. Sample content in English first; Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German follow, localised for each market. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Wariate Shifts. "Wariate" is the Japanese word for an allocation or rota; the company is otherwise international and plain-spoken.
- **Location:** 2nd floor, 18 Coldharbour Yard, Westmere (a fictional town, no country). USD prices in every language; no converted prices.
- **History:** founded in 2019. Hanna Rautio ran a bakery floor for nine years, and the week was still a paper sheet written in pencil before the shutters went up. Tomás Aguilar co-founded the company so that the same sheet could be laid out, shared and changed in one place.
- **What it is:** rota software for shops: lay out the week, collect availability, swap shifts, cover sick days, hours for payroll.
- **Audience:** managers of independent shops with 1 to 6 locations and 5 to 60 staff, including shops that open early and still keep the rota on paper or in a spreadsheet.
- **Tone:** Calm and plain, like a manager who used to write the week by hand before the shutters went up. Short sentences, shop-floor detail, and only the hours, prices and steps the sheet actually needs. No guarantees, statistics, awards, real shops or real brands.

## Story
Wariate Shifts was founded in 2019 in Westmere, on the second floor of 18 Coldharbour Yard. Hanna Rautio had run a bakery floor for nine years, and the week was still a paper sheet written in pencil before the shutters went up. Tomás Aguilar co-founded the company with her so that same sheet could be laid out, shared and changed in one place. The office is still at Coldharbour Yard. The work is still the week a manager has to finish before opening.

The product is rota software for independent shops. It lays out the week, collects availability, lets colleagues swap a shift, covers a sick day, and turns the hours into something ready for payroll. A week can start from last week's pattern. People go into the gaps, and the week is published to everyone's phone. Staff mark the days they can work, a reminder goes out on Thursday, and the answers land on the rota grid. A colleague offers a shift, another accepts, and the manager approves once. A sick day is marked open, free and qualified people are asked, and the first yes fills it. Clock-in is at the counter, breaks are added up, and the week can be exported by Sunday night. It is for managers with 1 to 6 locations and 5 to 60 staff, including shops that open early and still keep the rota on paper or in a spreadsheet.

The day at Coldharbour Yard follows those shops. Support opens at 07:00, Monday to Saturday, and runs until 19:00. The office is open Monday to Friday, 08:30 to 17:30. The first demo is at 10:00, setup sessions run at midday, and the 13:30 and 16:00 demos follow. Each demo is 60 minutes, by video call, or at the shop if it is in Westmere. A booking is answered within 1 working day. A draft of next week's rota goes out by email before 17:30, within 1 working day of the demo. The first month on any plan is free after the demo. Nothing is charged automatically, and a plan can be cancelled any month. Amara Okonkwo leads the demos, Lukas Brenner runs setup and onboarding, Mei-Lin Ho covers the early support shift, Rosa Ferreira designs the product, and Yusuf Demir looks after shop accounts.
