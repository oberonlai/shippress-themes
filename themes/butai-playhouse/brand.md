# Butai Playhouse - brand brief

Repertory playhouse in Easthaven with two hundred seats, five plays a season (September to May) and season tickets as well as single performances. "Butai" is the Japanese word for stage; the playhouse itself is international and contemporary. Sample content in English, Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German, localised for each market. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Butai Playhouse. "Butai" means stage.
- **Location:** 14 Lantern Row, Easthaven (fictional), a former brick tram depot converted into a playhouse. Prices in euros (EUR) in every language; no converted prices.
- **History:** founded in 2009 by Tomas Reyes (artistic director) and Margit Ellison (executive director) in the old tram depot. 2014 the rehearsal room was added, 2019 the under-26 season began, 2024 new seats were installed in the stalls.
- **Today:** a seven-person staff and a resident company of eight actors each season; the next play is rehearsed by day while the current one plays at night.
- **Audience:** regular playgoers, season subscribers, under-26s and schools in Easthaven. Theme buyers: independent theatres, theatre companies and small performing-arts venues that sell seasons.
- **Tone:** plain, warm, a little dry, theatre-literate. Say what plays when, where to sit and what it costs. No awards, no reviews quoted, no "best", no guarantees.

## The house
- 200 seats: stalls rows A-J of 14 (140) and circle rows K-M of 20 (60); four wheelchair spaces in row J; end-on stage 9.5 m wide; no seat more than eleven rows from the stage. Every seat is one price.

## A day at the playhouse (daily routine)
10:00 rehearsal room (the next play) - 12:00 box office opens - 16:00 technical checks on stage - 18:30 doors and bar - 19:25 beginners called - 19:30 curtain - about 21:45 house lights up. The same daily routine runs five evenings a week.

Supporting: vertical section label at the right edge (syuz'gen) and the scattered-baseline wordmark.
