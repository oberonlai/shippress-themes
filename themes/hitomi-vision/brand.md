# Hitomi Vision: brand

Independent optometry clinic for office workers and school-age children. Fictional; no real people, media or brands.

## Story
Hitomi Vision is an independent optometry clinic on the second floor of 14 Linden Row, Northgate. Dr. Elena Marsh founded it in 2011 for two groups who spend long hours at a fixed distance: office workers and school-age children. The rooms have stayed small on purpose. Three exam rooms and a frame room are enough for a sixty-minute visit that is not rushed between other appointments.

The work starts with what a person already notices. An adult may have a headache by four o'clock, blur after a screen, or eyes that burn by the end of a meeting. A child may skip lines, hold a book close, or say the board is soft. Those reports are symptoms, not a diagnosis. The exam measures refraction, how the two eyes work together at a real working distance, and the health of the eye, including the tear film. A plan comes after the measurements, in the same visit.

The clinic is still independent, and the same prices are quoted in every language, in US dollars. Frames begin at $160 and lenses are quoted only after the exam, because the lens has to match the distance the person actually uses. Dr. Marsh directs the clinic and sees adults for binocular vision. Colleagues cover children's vision, dry eye and contact lenses, and screen and workplace vision. A corrected case on this site is one person's account. Results differ, and the clinic does not promise that another person will finish in the same place.

## The 60-minute exam
1. History and symptoms, 10 minutes: what you notice, when it starts, and the distances you actually use.
2. Refraction, 15 minutes: the lens powers that clear distance and near, including a near add when one is needed.
3. Binocular vision and working distance, 10 minutes: how the two eyes work together at the desk or the screen, not only at the chart.
4. Eye health and tear film imaging, 10 minutes: the surface, the lids, and a look inside the eye.
5. Explanation and plan, 15 minutes: what was measured, what to change, and whether a follow-up within six months belongs in the plan.
