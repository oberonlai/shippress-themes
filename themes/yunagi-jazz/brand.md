# Yunagi Jazz: brand

## Name and place
Yunagi Jazz is a fictional open-air jazz festival held on one harbour lawn for one summer weekend. Venue: Harbour Lawn, Saltmere Quay, Port Avel (fictional, international-neutral). One stage only: the Lawn Stage. Box office: tickets@yunagijazz.example; general email hello@yunagijazz.example; phone +00 44 555 0188 (fictional).

## Location
Harbour Lawn at Saltmere Quay in Port Avel, a fictional harbour town. Getting there: boat shuttle from Old Town Pier (12 minutes, every 20 minutes from 15:00 to 23:40; last boat 23:40 Saturday, 22:40 Sunday), tram line 4 to Saltmere Quay, bike racks for 300 at Gate B.

## Founded
2016, by Margit Aalto. 2027 is the twelfth weekend: Saturday 21 and Sunday 22 August 2027. Capacity is 2,400 people per evening, and 600 paper lanterns are strung over the lawn and lit at sunset.

## Audience
People who like live jazz outdoors without the crowd: couples, friends, families with children, regulars who return each August. Buyers of the theme: music festival organisers and promoters selling weekend tickets.

## Tone
Calm, specific and unhurried, like a small seaside festival that prefers exact times to promotion. Plain logistics, acoustic detail and quiet warmth; no hype, no superlatives, no invented awards or sponsors.

## Story
Yunagi Jazz began in 2016 as an evening for friends on a patch of grass beside the harbour, with one stage, one set an hour and a string of paper lanterns. It has kept that shape: one stage set against the water, fourteen sets of fifty minutes across two evenings, and nothing playing over anything else.

The festival stays small on purpose. Attendance is capped at 2,400 a night, there are no video screens or sponsor tents, and every ticket is sold by our own box office so that prices stay fair and there are no resellers. Audiences bring blankets or borrow one of 400 low chairs, and many arrive by the harbour boat from Old Town Pier.

The 2027 weekend is the twelfth. The programme runs from a nine-piece street brass band at half past four on Saturday to a solo double bass at half past ten, with the lanterns lit by hand at twenty to nine on both nights.
