# Tabiji Clinic - brand brief

Travel-health and vaccination clinic in Vancouver, Canada. Sample content in English, Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German, localised for each market. All names, places, people, prices and numbers are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Tabiji Clinic. "Tabiji" is a Japanese word for the road of a journey; the clinic is otherwise international and modern.
- **Location:** 2F, 418 Water Street, Vancouver, BC V6B 1B6 (fictional unit), four minutes on foot from Waterfront Station.
- **Founded:** 2014 by Dr. Lena Okoye after ten years in expedition medicine.
- **People:** see below; seven named staff.
- **Audience:** adults, families and groups who are about to travel abroad: safari and backpacking trips, long stays for study or work, school and employer groups.
- **Tone:** The clinic speaks in a calm, candid, and clinically precise voice that avoids sensationalism, marketing jargon, and medical alarmism. Its copy prioritizes practical itinerary planning, unhurried care, and clear documentation in natural, measured English.
- **Prices:** CAD in every language.

## History
Tabiji Clinic was founded in 2014 by Dr. Lena Okoye after a decade of medical practice in expedition travel and remote wilderness environments. Established on the second floor of 418 Water Street in Vancouver, just a four-minute walk from Waterfront Station, the clinic was envisioned as a tranquil space dedicated to thoughtful travel-health preparation. Dr. Okoye recognized that travellers were frequently underserved by rushed consultations, leaving them anxious about foreign health requirements.

Instead of distributing generic, exhaustive checklists of every conceivable tropical illness, our clinical philosophy focuses entirely on the traveller's specific journey. We carefully evaluate the exact itinerary, including arrival dates, regional altitudes, rural stopovers, and accommodations, to determine precisely which immunisations and prophylactic treatments are necessary. Every initial appointment is allocated a full sixty minutes to ensure comprehensive assessment, gentle vaccine administration, and adequate post-injection rest.

Pharmaceutical rigor and meticulous international recordkeeping underpin every aspect of our care. Directed by pharmacist Samuel Achterberg, our temperature-monitored cold chain maintains the integrity of all vaccine supplies from storage to administration. Each administered dose is officially documented, signed, and stamped in recognized international vaccination booklets, supporting compliant passage across international borders and regulatory checkpoints worldwide.

## A day at the clinic
A routine day starts at 9:00 with the cold-chain check by the pharmacist, then back-to-back one-hour first appointments, short follow-up dose visits in the gaps, and a daily record review so every booklet leaves signed and stamped. The clinic closes at 18:00 on weekdays and 13:00 on Saturday. Group clinics run on location on set days.

## How an appointment hour is spent
- 01 | Review (20 min) — Comprehensive itinerary analysis, route mapping, and past immunisation assessment.
- 02 | Doses (25 min) — Gentle administration of required vaccines followed by a fifteen-minute rest period.
- 03 | Record (15 min) — Documentation, official signing, and stamping of your international vaccination booklet.
