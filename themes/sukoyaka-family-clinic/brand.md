# Sukoyaka Family Clinic — brand brief

Design plan row: b6-7 (family medical clinic, general practice). Built in English first and localised into zh-TW, ja, fr and de (the same mechanism as Hokuto Driving School 1.1.0, Kasumi Mortgage and Hagane Machinery). The brand world stays the same in every language; the wording of appointments, insurance and opening hours follows each market (a Taiwanese family-medicine clinic with registration and National Health Insurance cards, a Japanese neighbourhood internal-medicine clinic, a French cabinet de medecine generale, a German Hausarztpraxis).
All names, places, people and figures are fictional. Medical copy never promises a cure or an outcome: it describes what the clinic does, how long things take and who to call.

## Concept
- **Name:** Sukoyaka Family Clinic. *Sukoyaka* is a word the founder learned from a Japanese colleague during her training: healthy, and growing well. She liked that it describes a whole life, not a single illness.
- **Location:** 18 Linden Row, Ashford Green, a fictional town of terraced streets, a primary school, a market square and a riverside park. A two-storey brick building on the corner by the bakery: reception and waiting room, four consulting rooms, a treatment room and a small dispensary on the ground floor; the nurses' room and a quiet room upstairs.
- **Founded:** 1998, by **Dr Ellen Hart**, with one consulting room and one nurse. Moved to Linden Row in 2009; a second doctor since 2017.
- **Today:** two doctors, four nurses, three receptionists, about 4,800 registered patients from newborns to a 101-year-old.
- **Audience:** families in Ashford Green and the villages around it: parents with babies and school children, working adults who need an early or late appointment, older people living with long-term conditions, and the people who look after them.
- **Tone:** calm, plain and kind. Times, steps and names before adjectives. Never "best care", "guaranteed", "miracle" or "cure". We would rather say "most appointments start within ten minutes of the booked time" than "no waiting".

## People
- **Dr Ellen Hart** — founder and family doctor, 58. Trained in general practice; special interest in long-term conditions (diabetes, blood pressure, asthma) and in older patients. Still does the Thursday home visits herself.
- **Dr Tomas Lindqvist** — family doctor since 2017, 39. Children's health, sports injuries and the travel and vaccination clinic. Runs the Saturday morning surgery.
- **Ruth Okafor** — lead nurse since 2004. Vaccinations, wound care, the health check programme and the asthma reviews; trains new nurses.
- **The nursing team** — Ruth with nurses Hana Becker, Lucia Moreno and Sam Patel; the reception team led by Grace Whitfield.

## A day at Linden Row
- 07:30 The phone line and online booking open; Grace sets aside same-day appointments for children and urgent problems.
- 08:00 Morning surgery: ten-minute and twenty-minute appointments, blood tests in the treatment room until 10:00.
- 12:30 Doctors call back patients who asked for a phone consultation; the dispensary prepares the afternoon prescriptions.
- 14:00 Vaccination and health-check clinics run by the nurses; Thursday is home-visit afternoon.
- 16:00 Afternoon surgery until 19:00 on Monday and Wednesday, until 18:00 on other weekdays.
- 19:00 The last results are filed; anything that cannot wait goes to the on-call doctor.

## Signature
A neighbourhood family doctor for every age: a soft daylight photograph of the calm waiting room beside one plain sentence and a slim opening-hours line; a visit from reception to prescription as one vertical path with four photographed stops; the two named doctors and the nurses; and opening hours, vaccinations and health checks as plain lists further down.

## Palette (low chroma; photographs carry the colour)
- linen #F6F4EF (ground), stone #ECE8E0 (panels), paper #FBFAF7, ink #23292A (text), quiet #5E6763 (secondary text), oitake #6A8372 (the one muted accent: links, rules, labels, one button), pale-oitake #D9E1DB, mist #B7BFBA.

## Type
- Literata (display: headings, the clinic name, times and figures) over Source Sans 3 (body, labels, lists). Largest size 4.5rem for the first-screen sentence, never centred.
- Traditional Chinese and Japanese headings in Noto Serif TC / Noto Serif JP at about 0.75 of the Latin size; CJK body line height 1.85. French and German use hyphens:auto.

## Pages and contents
- **Home** — quiet first screen (text left, waiting-room photograph right, opening-hours line), a welcome from Dr Hart with two photographs, the visit path (reception, consultation, tests, dispensary), the two doctors and the nursing team, care for every age, opening hours / vaccinations / health checks as lists, journal, how to find us.
- **Care** (/care/) — what the clinic sees: everyday illness, long-term conditions, children, women's health, minor procedures, mental health first conversations; what we refer onwards.
- **Your visit** (/your-visit/) — booking, arriving, the consultation, tests and results, prescriptions; what to bring.
- **Doctors and nurses** (/team/) — the two doctors, Ruth and the nurses, reception; who to ask for what.
- **Families** (/families/) — babies and young children, school years, working age, later life, carers; with patient stories told with permission.
- **Vaccinations and checks** (/vaccinations-and-checks/) — the vaccination calendar, travel vaccines, health checks by age, the morning blood-test timeline.
- **About** (/about/) — the story since 1998, how we work, a day at Linden Row, our promises.
- **Contact** (/contact/) — address, phone, hours, booking, what to do out of hours; the clinic details are one synced pattern.
- **Newsletter** (/newsletter/) and **Journal** (/journal/, the posts page).
