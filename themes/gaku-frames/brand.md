# Gaku Frames - brand brief

A picture framing shop at a street-level workshop in the fictional Cooper's Yard, Halden, where the bench is visible from the counter. Sample content in English first; Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German follow, localised for each market. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Gaku Frames. "Gaku" is the Japanese word for a frame; the shop itself is international and modern, with no Japanese setting.
- **Idea:** a picture is finished by its frame, and we fit the frame to the wall it will hang on.
- **Location:** 27 Cooper's Yard, Halden (fictional). USD prices in every language; no converted prices.
- **History:** founded in 2011 by Mira Haldane, who opened the shop with one bench, one mitre saw and six woods. Every 45-degree corner is still cut on the same saw and joined by hand with corner clamps.
- **Today:** independent; seven people; the bench is in full view from the front counter; fittings by appointment, 30 minutes.
- **Audience:** people with a print, drawing, photograph or map that needs a frame for a particular wall: home owners, renters moving in, gift givers, small galleries and offices, and anyone re-framing something inherited.
- **Tone:** calm, specific and grounded in the craft. Exact dimensions, woods, mounts and wall placement; no decorative exaggeration, no guaranteed results, awards, press, ratings or customer counts.

- Conservation mount (acid-free board, hand-cut bevel, hinged print): +$28. Museum glass (UV-filtering, low reflection, per frame): +$65. Delivery and hanging within 25 km, up to 3 frames: $45.
- Walnut and gilt mouldings: +20% on the frame price. Turnaround 10 working days; express 3 working days, +$40. Sample corners: borrow up to 6 for 7 days, free.

## Signature techniques (carried to inner pages)
1. A walnut picture frame with four visible 45-degree mitre joints (four different shades per border, 32 px with a 12 px grey mount) holding butted low-key photographs and one centred 24 px label; every inner page head repeats it with one or two photos.
2. Mitre hairlines: two crossing 1 px 45-degree lines behind sections, one tinted brass, plus a vertical rule at the right margin with a small rotated caption; section headlines split into a giant uppercase word and an indented "/" second line.
3. A dark "On the bench" card (565 x 196, 191 px photo, brass highlight line, "[ -> ]") docked over the frame's corner and reused as the closing booking card, with 12 px grey pills for the menu and the tags.
Scroll: headline lines rise in a stagger, masonry photos wipe in as a mosaic, and the hero prints and one framed workshop band drift at different speeds behind their mounts.
Reference moods: a photographer's portfolio of butted black-and-white pictures for the frame window and pills; a creative studio's diagonal hairlines, split headline and docked dark card.
