# Tsubasa College - brand brief

An aircraft maintenance training college that prepares people to release an aircraft back to service: an eighteen-month licence-track programme taught on real aircraft in two working hangars. Sample content in English, Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German, localised for each market. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Tsubasa College.
- **Location:** Hangar Road 4, Carrow Field Aerodrome, Westmere (a fictional address). Email admissions@tsubasa-college.example, phone +00 41 7720 1998. Fees in euros (€) in every language.
- **History:** founded in 1998 by Henrik Aalto in a single hangar at Carrow Field; moved into two working hangars in 2009 (Hangar 1 for airframe and structures, Hangar 2 for turboprop and jet airframes); Henrik retired in 2019 and Ingrid Solberg became principal. The founding principle has not changed: technical competence is built on the hangar floor, and every task is signed and countersigned.
- **Audience:** school leavers, career changers (car mechanics, electricians), working mechanics who need a single systems module, and people returning to aviation, all aged 18 or over, who want to sit the national aviation authority's licence examinations with a documented task history behind them.
- **Tone:** calm, disciplined and technically precise. Plain sentences, no promotion. The college does not issue licences and does not guarantee results; licence examinations are sat with the national aviation authority. No invented statistics, no awards, no press, no real brands, employers or people.

## A day at Tsubasa College (daily routine)
07:30 tool control and a toolbox check; 07:45 shift briefing and task allocation; 08:00-12:00 task cards on live airframes in the hangar; 12:45-15:00 technical theory in the workshop classrooms; 15:00-16:00 duplicate inspection, sign-off and tool count, and the hangar closes at 16:00. The routine is the same every teaching day, so the habits form before the licence examinations are sat.

## Signature techniques (carried to every page)

Focus-pull hangar window (reference: the aftermarket-service page of mhi.com): one documentary photograph whose sharp centre (64% of the width) is flanked by 18% bands of the same frame stretched and blurred; uppercase serif headline, a bracketed label and a 40x2 rule. Home: full-bleed hero with the headline over the lower left. Inner pages: the same window at 2.34:1 inside the container.
2. Isometric module field (reference: the home page of mhi.com): square photo tiles on one rotated plane (rotateX 52deg, rotateZ -34deg), each with a white pill tag rotated with the plane, one dark pill, set on a pale wash that turns into a slate band.
3. Slate cohort strip with a white notice card (reference: the news row of mhi.com): a slate band from the left viewport edge across the foot of the photograph, a white card with a thumbnail, date and a red chevron underline.
Supporting: red chevron bullet links in two columns, a two-tier header with a centred menu, a grey five-column sitemap footer, a 48px black back-to-top square; slow image parallax on the hero and the hangar band, reveal on scroll and counters for the facts.
