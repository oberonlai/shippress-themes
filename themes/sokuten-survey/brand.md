# Sokuten Survey - brand brief

Independent land surveying firm in Brookmere. Landing theme: one long home page, an anchor menu and one repeated call to action, "Request a visit". Sample content in English, Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Sokuten Survey. "Sokuten" is the Japanese word for a measured point; the firm is otherwise international and modern.
- **Location:** office and instrument room at 14 Ferrow Lane, Brookmere (fictional town; only the address format is localised).
- **Founded:** 2009 by Ines Calloway after twelve years with a county survey office.
- **Audience:** owners of house plots and gardens, builders who need setting-out, and people about to buy a small piece of land within 40 km of the office.
- **Tone:** Write as someone speaking at the gate: short sentences, the pin, the stake, the time of day, and the price that is already published. Say what the hour includes, and do not add awards, press, or a result the visit has not earned.

## Story
Sokuten Survey is an independent land surveying firm at 14 Ferrow Lane, Brookmere. Ines Calloway opened the office in 2009 after twelve years with a county survey office, because she wanted the person who walks a plot to be the person who signs its plan. That is still how the work is done. Ines signs every boundary plan. The firm takes house plots, garden boundaries, builders' setting-out and small land purchases within 40 km of the office, and it does not stretch past that on a fixed fee.

A quote is not the first step. The visit is. Priya Nandakumar books the hour, and Hana Sørli reads the deeds and any old plan before the team leaves the lane. On the ground, Tomasz Reyes runs the total station and Felix Marlowe sets the stakes, the string and the pin caps. The hour lasts 60 minutes and has five stages. Most of those minutes are spent walking the line with the owner, looking for pins already in the ground. The instrument comes out only after that walk.

Amara Okonjo draws every measured plan. Lukas Brandt takes the GNSS and topographic surveys, including slopes and the larger plots, still inside the same 40 km. When a survey is booked, the measured plan follows within 10 working days as a PDF and one paper copy. The $180 visit fee is credited in full if that booking is made within 60 days. The office is open Mon–Fri 8:30–17:30. Field visits run Mon–Sat, with start times from 7:30 to 15:00. The firm is closed on Sundays and public holidays.

## The hour on the ground (the daily routine of a visit)
1. 0–10 min: At the gate, read the deeds and any old plan together.
2. 10–25 min: Walk the line with the owner and look for the pins already in the ground.
3. 25–40 min: Set the tripod, then shoot the pins and corners.
4. 40–50 min: Mark what was found with stakes, string and caps you can see.
5. 50–60 min: Fold out the field sketch and agree what, if anything, comes next.

Every visit is 60 minutes, 5 stages, 1 plan. The measured plan follows within 10 working days (PDF and one paper copy). A normal working day: office Mon-Fri 8:30-17:30; field visits Mon-Sat, start times 7:30-15:00; closed Sundays and public holidays.
