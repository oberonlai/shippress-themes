# Minato Business Hotel — brand brief

Design plan row: b6-5 (business hotel, hospitality). Built in English first and localised into zh-TW, ja, fr and de at the client's request (decision of 2026-10-20, the same mechanism as Hokuto Driving School 1.1.0 and Kasumi Mortgage). The brand world below stays the same in every language; the wording of rooms, breakfast, invoices and travel follows each country (a Taiwanese business hotel, a Japanese business hotel, a French hôtel d'affaires, a German Businesshotel).
All names, places, people, prices and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Minato Business Hotel. The founder, a ship's engineer, sailed into Yokohama as a young man and learned the word *minato*, harbour. His line: "A hotel for people who travel for work should be a harbour: quiet water, a warm light, and somebody on the quay."
- **Location:** 14 Quay Street, Port Ellis, a fictional harbour city with an intercity station (Port Ellis Central), an old quay of converted warehouses and plane-tree streets. The hotel is the former shipping-line office, a grey stone building with a dusty rose door, four minutes on foot from the station's harbour exit.
- **Founded:** 1987, by **Arthur Penrose**, with twenty-two rooms. Grew to sixty rooms in 1998 when the station reopened, eighty-four rooms today; every room rebuilt in 2022 without closing.
- **Today:** 84 rooms on five floors, 31 staff, about 19,000 guests a year, more than half of them returning.
- **Audience:** engineers at the port, auditors and consultants, sales teams, doctors and teachers on placements and training weeks, companies that send people every month, the occasional family on the way to the ferry.
- **Tone:** calm, practical and specific. Times and minutes before adjectives. Never "luxury", "world-class" or "unforgettable". We would rather say "four minutes, flat all the way" than "perfectly located".

## People
- **Helen Penrose** — general manager since 2009, Arthur's daughter. Trained in hotels in Lyon and Hamburg. Took the minibars out and put desks in; checks two rooms herself every afternoon.
- **Daniel Okafor** — night manager since 2012. Knows every timetable in and out of Port Ellis; leaves a sandwich and a note for late arrivals.
- **Marta Kowalska** — breakfast since 2014, from a bakery in Gdańsk. Opens the breakfast room at six and packs a paper bag for the first train.
- **Sofia Lind** — head of housekeeping, a team of nine. Chose the mattresses in 2022 after a week on each.

## A day at Quay Street
- 05.30 Paper bags ready at the desk; 06.00 the breakfast room opens for the 6.10 intercity.
- 07.00 Daniel hands the night book to the day desk: early taxis, late arrivals, special requests.
- 10.00 Housekeeping floor by floor, windows open, linen counted twice.
- 15.00 Arrivals from the afternoon trains; Helen walks the corridors and checks two rooms at random.
- 23.00 The night desk: late check-ins, an ironed shirt, a sandwich after the last train.

## Photography
One STYLE for every image: high-key documentary photograph, 28mm lens, bright soft natural daylight, white linen, pale oak and warm stone greys with faint muted dusty rose, clean, calm and uncluttered, realistic, natural colours, no readable text, logos or signage. Rooms, lobby, breakfast, the street to the station, the harbour at dusk, hands at the desk and three calm portraits. No cut-outs.

## User aesthetic
Restrained and real: no poster look, no saturated colour fields, no cut-outs, no side rail, no giant centred slogan, no table, form or booking search on the first screen. Generous whitespace; photographs and narrative paragraphs do the work; one top menu.
