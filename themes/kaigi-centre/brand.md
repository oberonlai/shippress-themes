Conference centre for associations and corporate offsites at Halden Harbour (fictional, international-neutral). Sample content in English (stage 1: home page); Traditional Chinese, Japanese, French and German follow in stage 2. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Kaigi Centre. Five permanent rooms in a converted 1920s harbour customs warehouse, opened in 2009.
- **Location:** 12 Quay Street, Halden Harbour - 6 min walk from Halden Central station, tram line 2 stop "Quay Street". Prices in euros (EUR, €) in every language; no converted amounts.
- **Hours:** Office: Mon–Fri 9:00–18:00; Building: 7:00–23:00 on event days. Open day Sat 14 November 2026, hall tours 10:00-15:00, no booking needed.
- **Terms:** 48-hour provisional hold, free; 30% deposit on contract, balance 14 days after the event; half day = 60% of full day.
- **Audience:** association boards and congress organisers, society training leads, corporate offsite planners.
- **Voice:** The voice of Kaigi Centre is calm, measured, and operationally precise, communicating with the understated authority of an established harbour venue. It avoids marketing hyperbole and vague superlatives, prioritizing practical details such as room dimensions, exact schedules, and thorough preparation.

## Story
Kaigi Centre was founded in 2009 within a converted 1920s customs warehouse on the waterfront at Halden Harbour. Originally constructed for maritime storage and cargo inspection, the brick warehouse was carefully repurposed under the direction of Ingrid Halvorsen into an integrated conference venue. The architectural restoration preserved the building's authentic industrial character, maintaining the exposed red brick masonry, original structural timber trusses, and tall warehouse windows that admit calm, natural daylight across the halls throughout the working day.

The centre was established to host association congresses, scientific symposia, and corporate leadership teams seeking a quiet, focused environment for assembly. Rather than dividing the historic interior into temporary partitioned spaces, the facility was designed around five permanent rooms connected by wide circulation corridors. Today, the network accommodates diverse event formats, from private sixteen-seat meetings in the harbour-facing Quay Room to four-hundred-and-twenty-delegate plenaries in Meridian Hall, supported by an on-site kitchen and dedicated technical infrastructure.

Throughout our history, operations have been defined by an attentive and punctual house crew. Every gathering is coordinated directly by our permanent staff, who verify audio-visual systems, set room layouts, and lay out presentation materials before the doors open each morning. Located a six-minute walk from Halden Central station, Kaigi Centre combines practical maritime heritage with disciplined, dependable event execution.
