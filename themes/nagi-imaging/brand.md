# Nagi Imaging — brand brief

Design plan row: b16-1 (diagnostic imaging centre, type health). Stage one: the home page in English. Inner pages and the other languages follow. All names, places, people and figures are fictional. Medical copy never promises a diagnosis or an outcome.

## Concept
- **Name:** Nagi Imaging, an outpatient imaging room. "Nagi" is the Japanese word for a calm sea; here it stands for the quiet of a darkened reading room.
- **Location:** 14 Calder Quay, Grand Canal Dock, Dublin 2 (fictional). Phone +353 1 555 0148; hello@nagi-imaging.example; referrals@nagi-imaging.example. Four-minute walk from the tram stop.
- **Founded:** 2016 by Dr. Amira Haddad and Tomás Oyelaran with one CT scanner; MRI added in 2019.
- **Services:** X-ray, ultrasound, DEXA bone density, mammography with breast ultrasound, CT and MRI for patients referred by nearby clinics, with a signed report to the referring doctor.
- **Audience:** GPs, physiotherapists and clinics within a few kilometres, and their patients.
- **Tone:** Calm, precise, and understated, the voice communicates with clinical clarity and quiet confidence. It presents concrete workflows, exact turnaround times, and verified numbers without marketing exaggeration or clinical over-promising.

## Story
Nagi Imaging was founded in 2016 at 14 Calder Quay in Grand Canal Dock, Dublin 2, by Dr. Amira Haddad and Tomás Oyelaran. Beginning as a compact outpatient imaging room equipped with a single computed tomography scanner, the practice was established around a simple but urgent question: could an imaging report reach a referring general practitioner before their clinic closed for the evening? Rather than accepting multi-day waiting periods for routine diagnostic scans, the founders designed an operational workflow focused exclusively on serving nearby medical practices.

In 2019, Nagi Imaging expanded its diagnostic capabilities by installing a dedicated magnetic resonance imaging suite. Throughout this growth, Dr. Haddad and Tomás maintained an uncompromised commitment to diagnostic accuracy and local accessibility, instituting an independent double-reading protocol for every CT and MRI examination. By partnering closely with community health centres, physiotherapists, and family doctors across Dublin, the room established a dependable diagnostic link that eliminated administrative friction and expedited clinical decision-making.

Today, the practice continues to operate from Calder Quay under its founding same-day principle. Patients arriving in the morning complete their examinations in a calm, low-key setting, and every scan completed before 15:00 is thoroughly interpreted, verified, and delivered to the referring physician by 18:00. This deliberate focus on same-day reporting allows doctors to adjust treatment plans promptly, reassuring patients and removing the anxiety of prolonged waiting.

## Report times (one rule everywhere)
Scans finished before 15:00 are reported by 18:00 the same working day. Scans after 15:00 and Saturdays are reported by 12:00 the next working day. Every CT and MRI is read by two radiologists. For urgent findings the radiologist phones the referrer within the hour.
