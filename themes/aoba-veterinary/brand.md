# Aoba Veterinary — brand brief

Design plan row: b8-1 (veterinary clinic for dogs and cats, type health). Stage one: the home page in English. Inner pages and the other four languages follow. All names, places, people and figures are fictional. Medical copy never promises a cure or an outcome.

## Concept
- **Name:** Aoba Veterinary Clinic, a small general practice for dogs and cats.
- **Location:** 214 Alder Street, Fairhaven (fictional town); phone +1 555 0142 300; hello@aobavet.example; 6 parking spaces.
- **Founded:** 2014 by Dr. Elena Varga after twelve years in a city referral hospital, to run a practice where an animal is seen without a rushed consultation.
- **Services:** general practice, surgery and orthopaedics, dentistry and imaging, feline medicine; in-house blood tests, digital X-ray and ultrasound; vaccinations, microchipping, senior care.
- **Audience:** dog and cat owners in the neighbourhood, from first-time puppy and kitten visits to long-term care of older animals.
- **Tone:** calm, concrete, specific about the animal, the person, the time and the fee. No guarantees, awards, slogans or urgency the facts do not support.

## Hours
Mon–Fri 9:00–12:30 and 15:30–19:00; Sat 9:00–12:30 and 15:30–17:00; Sun and public holidays closed. Urgent same-day slots each weekday. Out of hours: the regional emergency hospital.

## Fees (USD, tax included, written estimate before treatment)
First consultation (30 min, full examination) $65; Follow-up consultation (15 min) $45; Annual vaccination (dog DHPP or cat FVRCP), incl. check $58; Microchip and registration $49; Senior blood panel $110; Cat spay / neuter from $240; Dog spay / neuter from $380; Dental scale and polish under anaesthesia from $320.

## Visit flow
Book (30-minute first consultation) → Settle in (cats in their own waiting room) → Examine and explain (written estimate before treatment) → Follow up (a nurse calls within 48 hours).

## Recovery stories (three, each a single case)
1. Biscuit, beagle, 7: cruciate ligament repair by Dr. Osei; 10 weeks from surgery to full walks with Malik's rehab plan.
2. Juniper, cat, 12: weight loss led to a thyroid condition, managed with daily medication and checks every 3 months (Dr. Nair).
3. Otto, Labrador, 8 months: swallowed a sock, removed by endoscopy (Dr. Ribeiro), home the same evening.
Every animal is different; the stories are not a promise of the same result.
