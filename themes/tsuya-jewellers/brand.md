# Tsuya Jewellers - brand brief

An independent jeweller (a small bench, a front room and a WooCommerce shop) in Port Ellery. Sample content in English first; Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German follow in stage two, localised rather than translated. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Tsuya Jewellers. "Tsuya" is the Japanese word for lustre or sheen; the bench is otherwise international and modern.
- **Location:** shop and workshop at 22 Wexley Street, Port Ellery (fictional). USD prices in every language; no converted prices.
- **History / founded:** 2011. Ines Marlow opened the bench with one conviction: jewellery should be made for the life it actually meets, not for a shop window.
- **Today:** six people at one bench, small runs of rings, chains and studs in recycled gold and silver, plus commissions and heirloom remakes.
- **Audience (who it is for):** people who want one ring for twenty years; couples who want two bands that do not match exactly; people bringing a family piece to be made into something they will actually wear.
- **Tone / voice:** The voice is calm, unadorned, and grounded in the tactile reality of the workshop, speaking about jewellery as durable daily companions rather than luxury status symbols. It describes materials, techniques, and timelines with quiet precision, avoiding hyperbole, ornamental prose, and sales jargon.
- **A day at the bench (routine):** Tuesday to Saturday the lamps go on at eleven; mornings are filing and setting, afternoons are fittings and bench talks; Thursday the shop stays open until eight; Sunday and Monday are bench days with the door closed.

## The story
Ines Marlow opened the bench at 22 Wexley Street in Port Ellery in 2011 with a simple conviction: jewellery should be made for the life it actually encounters. Rather than polishing pieces for display cases or distant occasions, she began shaping and forging rings in recycled gold and silver that were meant to be worn on an ordinary Tuesday. The workshop was established as an open bench where files, lamps, and bench pegs remained visible, anchoring every piece in daily studio practice.

Over the years, the workshop grew into a dedicated team of six craftspeople working in the same quiet room. Kwame Asante joined to set stones under the loupe, Ravi Menon took on the scale drawings and commission consultations, Lea Brandt brought precise hand engraving to signet faces, Sofia Duarte took charge of repairs and bench polishing, and Hana Okada began managing shop fittings. Together, they share tools and worktables, passing each piece from hand to hand from raw metal to final polish.

Today, Tsuya Jewellers produces small, disciplined runs alongside custom commissions and heirloom redesigns. Whether melting down an inherited bracelet into a pair of wedding bands or carving a custom wax model for a fitting, the bench works deliberately and transparently. Each piece is constructed with honest weight and balanced proportions, designed to collect gentle marks over decades of wear at the desk, on the bike, and at the sink without wearing out.

## Commission process (identical on every page)
1. Bench talk: A free forty-five-minute consultation at the workshop to discuss ideas, materials, and daily wear.
2. Drawing and quote: Detailed scale drawings and a firm quote delivered within 10 days, followed by a 40 % deposit upon approval.
3. Wax and fitting: A green wax model carved for a single fitting in the shop to test volume, proportions, and comfort.
4. Making and finishing: Casting in recycled metal, setting, filing, and bench finishing, completed within 6–8 weeks from drawing approval.
A 40 % deposit is paid when the drawing is approved. Stones are quoted separately at the drawing stage.
