# Kodama Sound - brand brief

A sound designer and composer studio for film and games in Halden Bay (fictional): original score, foley and re-recording mix. Sample content in English first; Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German follow in stage 2, localised for each market. All names, places, projects and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Kodama Sound. "Kodama" is an echo, the sound a place sends back; the studio itself is international and modern.
- **Location:** 14 Kiln Yard, Halden Bay (fictional coastal town). Halden Bay station, 7 minutes on foot; no visitor parking; bicycle rack in the yard.
- **Founded:** 2015 by Ines Valk, a field recordist turned composer, after recording a fishing harbour at night for a friend's short film.
- **Today:** seven people, two rooms (a 7.1 mix stage and a foley pit with four surfaces: gravel, wood, concrete, water) and one field kit that fits in one bag. 64 projects since 2015, 3,200 field recordings in the library.
- **Audience:** film directors, producers, game studios and audio leads. Theme buyers are freelance sound designers, composers and audio post houses pitching to directors and game studios.
- **Tone:** The voice is quiet, exact, and warm, relying on concrete numbers, specific hours, and technical detail rather than decorative adjectives. It speaks in a calm first-person plural, presenting the studio and its acoustic post-production process plainly. No guarantees, no awards, no real titles, clients, broadcasters or brands.

## Story
Kodama Sound was founded in 2015 in Halden Bay by Ines Valk, a field recordist turned composer. The studio began after Ines spent nine nights recording a quiet fishing harbour for a friend's short film, realising that dramatic cues should grow directly from the physical acoustic environment rather than synthetic presets. What started in a single room with a portable recorder soon established our core working method: capturing the unvarnished sonic reality of a location before writing the first bar of music.

As productions grew, the facility expanded to support complete soundtrack workflows under one roof. In 2018, we built a dedicated foley pit outfitted with four distinct surfaces—gravel, wood, concrete, and water—allowing performers to produce precise, tactile footsteps and prop movement. In 2021, we added a purpose-built 7.1 re-recording mix stage, followed in 2024 by our first fully adaptive game audio architecture spanning dozens of dynamic musical cues.

Today, seven people work together from 14 Kiln Yard across two control rooms, our live foley stage, and a field kit that packs into a single shoulder bag. Having completed sixty-four projects since 2015 and catalogued 3,200 field recordings across every hour of the day and night, we remain devoted to the quiet discipline of our craft. Every room and every landscape has an innate tone, and we still go out to record it first.

## Fixed numbers (identical on every page and in every language)
- Process, a typical short film takes 8 weeks:
- 01 Spot: Day 1, a 2-hour spotting session with the director to map every sound cue and thematic transition.
- 02 Record: Weeks 1–2, capturing original environmental field recordings and dedicated foley pit tracks.
- 03 Score: Weeks 2–6, developing thematic motifs and musical cues with two structured revision rounds.
- 04 Mix: Weeks 6–8, balancing dialogue, effects, and music on the 7.1 stage with the director in the room.
- 05 Deliver: Week 8, supplying complete stems, M&E tracks, and loudness-checked masters within 3 working days of sign-off.
- Features take 12-16 weeks; games run in 2-week sprints per milestone; series take 3 weeks per episode.
- Rates (EUR, excluding VAT):
- Short film score (up to 20 min): **From €3,800**. Includes spotting session, thematic development, two rounds of revisions, and final stereo stems plus M&E.
- Feature film score: **From €18,000**. Full orchestral and acoustic scoring across 12–16 weeks, including spotting, two revision passes, and multichannel stems.
- Sound design and foley, short film: **From €2,900**. Custom environmental sound design and complete physical foley pit recording for short films up to 20 minutes.
- Foley stage day (artist + recordist): **€1,150 per day**. Dedicated day on our foley pit with both foley artist and recording engineer across gravel, wood, concrete, and water surfaces.
- Game audio, per 100 sound effects: **From €2,400**. Custom interactive audio assets delivered in 2-week milestone sprints, fully edited and tagged for implementation.
- Re-recording mix, short film (stereo + 5.1): **€2,200**. Complete theatrical re-recording mix in stereo and 5.1 surround on our 7.1 mix stage, with director playback.
- Mixer day rate: **€980 per day**. Attended mix session with re-recording mixer on the 7.1 stage for specialized balance passes, conform, or stem preparation.
- Included in every project: a spotting session, two rounds of revisions, stems and M&E delivery.
