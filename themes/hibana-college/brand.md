## Story

Hibana College opened in 2011 at 27 Slipway Road, Port Aldren, where the workshop and the office still share one address. Marit Solberg had worked in shipyard fitting. She left that trade to teach adults who had never held a torch, and the college has kept that purpose: a welding and metal-fabrication school for people changing jobs. The day runs on a steel bench, a drawing, and an instructor close enough to watch the visor come down.

A Six-Month Course cohort is twelve students, with two instructors on the floor, one for every six. Twenty-four booths give each student a place to work. The course is twenty-six weeks, Monday to Thursday, seven taught hours a day, which is seven hundred and twenty-eight hours. Three cohorts start each year, on 11 January, 3 May and 6 September. Fridays are free on purpose, because many students keep a part-time job while they train. Office hours are Monday to Friday, 9:00–17:00. The day course runs 8:30–16:30. Evening Foundations meets on Tuesday and Thursday, 18:00–21:00.

The six months move in order: bench and safety, MIG on plate, stick in position, TIG on steel and stainless, fabrication, then test pieces and placement. Each week ends with one marked piece on the review rack. In the last month a visiting independent inspector sees the test plates, and students spend two weeks on placement in a local shop. Claire Dubois arranges that placement. The college teaches the trade and arranges the placement. It cannot promise a job or a test result. Open Bench, free on the first Saturday of each month from 10:00 to 13:00, is where a new student first tries a torch under supervision.

## How a cohort begins

1. Open Bench: free on the first Saturday of the month, 10:00–13:00, try a torch under supervision.
2. Application: a one-page form, and no experience is needed.
3. Bench talk: 30 minutes with Claire Dubois and one instructor.
4. Day one: a $500 deposit secures a booth, and the kit is issued on the first morning.
