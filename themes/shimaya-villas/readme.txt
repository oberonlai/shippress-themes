Shimaya Villas is a calm, documentary-style WordPress theme designed for direct-booking island retreats and holiday house rentals. Built with clean sans typography and warm photographic windows, it showcases accommodation, direct booking inclusions, seasonal availability, and island hosts without third-party booking intermediaries. Features responsive block patterns, native theme.json styling, and streamlined reservation hold workflows.

== Description ==

A WordPress block theme from ShipPress (https://github.com/oberonlai/shippress-themes). The home page opens on a framed photograph window with the headline on a solid panel at its corner and a dark glass hold-dates bar docked on its foot, under a centred two-row header and a coloured promo ribbon. It continues with a centred statement and three narrative paragraphs, a full-width morning photograph band, three house cards, a grey-out list of the five season periods that swaps a small portrait and a large photograph together, five ruled rows of what booking direct adds, two arrival cards, the hosts, one way to hold dates and the journal. See README.md for the design notes.

== Installation ==

1. Download shimaya-villas.zip from https://github.com/oberonlai/shippress-themes/releases/download/themes/shimaya-villas.zip
2. In WordPress admin, go to Appearance > Themes > Add New Theme > Upload Theme, choose the zip and click Install Now.
3. Activate Shimaya Villas.

On activation the theme sets itself up as a ready-to-use website (inc/demo-import.php): it creates its pages with all their text and images, journal entries with categories and tags, the menu, and sets the front page and posts page. It never changes or deletes anything you wrote, and running it again creates nothing twice.

The scroll effects (assets/sp-scroll.css and sp-scroll.js) respect prefers-reduced-motion and show the final state without JavaScript. The newsletter and contact slots use Contact Form 7, WPForms, MailPoet or Mailchimp for WP when active; without a plugin they fall back to an email link.

The island, the company, its people, address, telephone number, email and prices are fictional.
