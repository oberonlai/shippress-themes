# Hojo Brokers - brand brief

Independent commercial insurance broker for small and mid-size firms with physical premises (warehouses, workshops, distributors), in Calder Bay, a fictional port town. Sample content in English, Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Japanese, French and German, localised for each market. All names, places, people and figures are fictional.

## Concept
- **Name:** Hojo Brokers. A broker that reads a firm's risks, on site, before it sells a policy.
- **Location:** 3rd floor, 18 Quayside Road, Calder Bay (fictional). Telephone +00 41 2290 6604, email review@hojobrokers.example. Prices in euro.
- **Founded:** 2009 by Mara Hojo, who started with two clients and a borrowed desk above a hauliers' yard.
- **Hours:** Mon-Fri 08:30-17:30; site visits Mon-Sat by appointment (Sat 09:00-13:00); closed Sundays and public holidays. The claims line is answered in office hours, the same day.
- **Audience:** owners and finance managers of firms with a yard, a warehouse or a workshop: cold-store and food distributors, timber and building supplies, print and packaging workshops, light manufacturers, wholesalers.
- **Tone:** calm, specific and technically precise, rooted in the physical site and the policy wording. No marketing hyperbole, no promised outcomes, no invented statistics.
- **One call to action (everywhere):** "Ask for a cover review".

## History
Hojo Brokers was established in 2009 by Mara Hojo on the 3rd floor of 18 Quayside Road. It was built for small and mid-size businesses with physical premises, and from the first year it rejected telephone sales in favour of walking a client's site before placing anything.

The team grew with surveyors and brokers who could read racking, sprinklers, kilns and contracts alongside the owner. Grounding every assessment in a visit rather than a desk estimate turned up the same gaps again and again: stock declared at an average, indemnity periods shorter than a rebuild, customers' goods on the floor that nobody had insured.

Today six people work from Calder Bay. The firm stays independent, comparing schedules and wordings from several insurers and showing every fee and commission in writing.

## A day at the firm (the daily routine)
08:30 phones open and the day's visits are confirmed. 09:00 brokers and the surveyor leave for site visits (a visit is 90 minutes on the owner's floor). 12:30 notes from the morning are typed up while still fresh. 14:00 cover maps and wording reads are written. 16:00 client calls, renewals and certificates. 17:30 the office closes; the claims line is answered until then, the same day. On Saturday visits run 09:00-13:00 by appointment. The routine repeats Monday to Friday; Sunday and public holidays are closed.

## The review (three steps)
1. **Visit and read** - a 90-minute walk of the yard and building with the owner, plus the current schedule and wordings.
2. **Map the gaps** - a written cover map within 10 working days: what is covered, what is not, what is underinsured.
3. **Placement explained** - a 60-minute meeting: options from 3-5 insurers, every exclusion read in plain words, commission disclosed. No obligation to place.

## Cases (no guaranteed outcomes)
- **Cold-store distributor, 14 staff** - stock declared at an average, not the peak season value; a seasonal stock clause was added.
- **Timber merchant, 2 yards** - the business interruption indemnity period was 6 months, a rebuilt drying kiln takes about 14; the period was lengthened.
- **Contract print workshop, 9 staff** - customers' paper and plates on site were not covered; they were added as goods in trust.
