## Name and place

## Founded

## Audience
School leavers and career changers who want a first qualification at sea, parents choosing a training college, and partner operators of coastal ferries, harbour tugs and short-sea cargo vessels that offer training berths.

## Voice
Calm and concrete, the way a harbour office would speak to an applicant on Pier 3: specific about weeks, dates and fees. Plain sentences. No guarantees of a licence, a job, a placement rate or a berth; no awards, no real companies.

## Story
Hatoba College was founded in 1987 at Pier 3, 4 Quay Road, Port Ellery. From the first intake the harbour was the classroom: paper charts, lines and engines were taught in sight of cranes, tugs and moored vessels. The pier, the timetable and the size of each intake have stayed small on purpose: eighteen cadets join in January, April and September.

Harbour Foundation, twelve weeks, is the course most cadets take first. It runs in five stages: shore school (weeks 1–4), simulator bridge (weeks 5–7), harbour watch (weeks 8–10), tug and pilot runs (week 11) and the assessment with berth confirmation (week 12). A training berth is confirmed in writing on the Friday of week 12, with partner operators of coastal ferries, harbour tugs and short-sea cargo vessels.

## A day (daily routine)
07:30 muster on the quay; morning chart work or a watch at the berth; 12:00 simulator debrief where each cadet explains the decision just made; 16:00 line handling with the tug crew, ropes and fenders; the office closes at 17:00. The daily routine keeps its shape; the week changes.
